### Sampler and λ

1. **Collapsed block.** Let η = log λ, m = n−3, t = λ²s, v = Wr and Pη = W + exp(2η)D′diag(1/t)D. The t prior is Exp(rate ½). The Gaussian prior on the m differences contributes λᵐ. Integrating θ, including its three flat polynomial directions, gives log density in η equal to (a₀+m)η − b₀ exp(η) − ½ log det Pη + ½v′Pη⁻¹v, up to a constant. The η Jacobian supplies the last power of λ. Retain the ordinary λ|θ draw with s integrated out, refresh t given the new λ and θ, take an invariant slice step, then immediately redraw θ at the new λ and t. One slice transition preserves the block law but retains dependence on the previous λ. The prototype has this ordering and uses a banded QR. Include an independent small-n quadrature reference for λ and stress the determinant at large λ.
2. **θ move.** With drift −(θ−μ), μ=P⁻¹Wr, and noise covariance 2P⁻¹ per unit time, implicit midpoint gives θ′=μ+r(θ−μ)+√(1−r²)P⁻¹ᐟ²ξ, r=(1−h/2)/(1+h/2), ξ standard normal. Its stationary Gaussian pair is exchangeable, so MH acceptance is one. At h=2 it is an independent conditional draw. Call it an *implicit preconditioned Langevin update on the augmented Gaussian conditional*. That meets “P-MALA-like” under the preconditioned interpretation. If Felipe requires the paper’s proximal proposal on the marginal θ target, use the hybrid and keep the exact θ refresh inside the collapsed block.
3. **λ prior.** For k=2 use Gamma(shape 2, rate sᵧ n⁻⁵ᐟ²), with sᵧ=1.4826 raw MAD(D³y)/√20. The stencil squares sum to 20, giving the Gaussian calibration under independent equal-variance errors and negligible signal differences. Under heteroscedasticity this is a response-unit scale, distinct from worst-location sparsity. Scaling y by a positive factor scales the rate likewise and λ inversely. At fixed V>0 and sᵧ>0 the prior probability of a fixed multiplicative oracle window depends only on sᵧ/V. This establishes a mass calculation, not posterior adaptation. Same-data sᵧ requires empirical-Bayes control, e.g. a theorem uniform in the rate multiplier on a compact set and high-probability containment. For ties use IQR then range. Constant responses require an external unit or a flagged descriptive fit. The prototype starts weak P at σ=0 there.

### Choosing σ

**4F.** For a known law use 1.1σ̄, where σ̄=τ(1−τ) maxᵢ 1/fᵢ(qᵢ), when EM is established at that value. Otherwise use an explicitly sufficient EM threshold from W01 Proposition 2 if applicable. Theorem B gives the squared-loss contraction rate up to logs at deterministic oracle-order λ under its other assumptions. A point-density margin alone does not establish global EM. In data, pilot the proposed 2τ(1−τ) times the maximum fitted residual quantile spacing divided by 2h, using the quadratic design and Hall–Sheather h. Include endpoints, reject crossings and sparse local tails, and return “F unavailable” on abstention. A pooled density can miss the least dense location. Convexity of population sparsity controls the symmetric quantile difference at a *given* location. It gives no one-sided guarantee for the fitted quadratic at a boundary.

**4P.** For a coherent prior, take IG(shape 1, scale L) restricted to [L,2L], with L chosen by the F rule above. The exact σ|θ draw is IG(n+1,L+Q(θ)) on that interval, followed by z|θ,σ. Choose L in advance for the theory arm. When L>q=Q(θ)/n by a fixed relative margin, the conditional density concentrates near L on relative scale 1/[n(1−q/L)]. Boundary concentration is acceptable if L encodes the intended minimum temperature. Report it and favor F when the two give indistinguishable fits. The conditional likelihood loss at Rq versus q is n(log R+1/R−1), or 46.33, 141.20 and 525.68 nats for the stated ratios at n=500. This is conditional on θ, or a population contrast at the truth. A fixed weak positive prior supplies only O(1) log odds at two fixed scales. A sufficiently concentrated smooth prior can move σ at finite n. A compact support restriction makes the proof-range requirement explicit.

### Referee answer

We learn σ when we want the data to set the units of check loss. Under the asymmetric Laplace working likelihood, the population scale criterion at the true quantile is log σ + σ₀/σ, where σ₀ is average expected check loss when finite. Its minimizer is σ₀. An inverse-gamma prior yields a one-draw conditional update and propagates uncertainty about that scale. Under misspecification, σ measures working-likelihood temperature. Its physical interpretation depends on the noise law.

Only Λ=λσ enters the conditional θ mode through Q(θ)+Λ‖Dθ‖₁. Even at fixed Λ, σ changes posterior spread. Learning σ optimizes a working-likelihood criterion and carries no general guarantee of nominal band coverage. For the paper’s three noise families σ₀ falls below the proof threshold. We therefore use fixed temperatures to connect the method to theory and compare the two choices on estimation error, width and coverage. Related work examines the separate covariance adjustment needed for inference in Bayesian quantile regression. [Yang, Wang and He (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1111/insr.12114)
