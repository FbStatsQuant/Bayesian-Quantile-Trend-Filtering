# Astra | E-07 individual sampler design memo

**Decision requested.** Pilot a common Gamma prior and a scale-mixture preconditioned Langevin move. Offer learned scale (P) for ordinary fitting and fixed scale (F) for calibration studies. All proposed prior changes, standardisation, and replacement of paper estimates or tables require HQ approval. Kernel and stopping changes preserve the mathematical target when exact, but can change finite-run results.

1. **Use Gamma(shape 2, rate n⁻⁵ᐟ²) on standardised data for both P and F, k=2.** Set d=median(y), s=IQR(y)/1.349, falling back to range(y) if the IQR vanishes. Fit x=(y−d)/s. Return θ=d+sθₓ, σ=sσₓ and λ=λₓ/s. For constant data, flag the undefined scale and return the constant as a descriptive fit only. No positive response-derived scale can be equivariant there. Translation and positive rescaling preserve x. Both the check loss Q and S=‖Dθ‖₁ have response units. The log penalty λS is dimensionless and the conditional MAP minimises Q+σλS. Integrating λ in regime (iii) gives Q/σ+(m+2)log(b₀+S) up to a constant, m=n−k−1. The raw Gamma rate is s n⁻⁵ᐟ². Explicitly select the driver's paper setting prior_scale="none".

   On the standard scale, balancing λ⁻¹ᐟ³ against V²λ⁵ᐟ³n⁻⁵ gives λ≈n⁵ᐟ²/V. For V>0 fixed, λ/n⁵ᐟ² has a fixed Gamma(2,1) prior, hence positive mass in every fixed multiplicative oracle interval. Shape 2 follows D1. **c=1 is a pilot choice**, with prior mode n⁵ᐟ² and conjugate posterior rate n⁻⁵ᐟ²+Sₓ. It encodes no estimate of V. E-02 §§1–3 measured the spread law and learned-λ slopes 2.54 (sin) and 2.14 (PQ), versus 0.745 for fixed rate. A compound Gamma adds a tail-index choice without demonstrated gain. Excessive smoothing of the VS bump is the main risk. Standardisation is empirical Bayes. W-01 Theorem B assumes deterministic λ,σ and (EM). A is open, A⁺ and learned-λ rates are targets. For optional k≠2, pilot the same numerical rule as an explicitly unvalidated fallback. Cost is one transform and the existing Gamma draw.

2. **P learns check-loss scale. F estimates worst-location sparsity.** For P, pending the VP decision, use IG(0.1,0.1) on σₓ. Given θₓ, write q=Qₓ/n. The collapsed conditional mean is (nq+0.1)/(n−0.9), with relative SD 1/√(n−1.9), about 4.5% at 500. The shift from q is (0.1+0.9q)/(n−0.9), potentially material for tiny q. A proper concentrated prior *can* move this conditional substantially. For a desired mode t, choose bσ=t(n+aσ+1)−nq if positive (sensitivity identity only). A weak prior cannot reliably bridge the simulation gap of up to about 21-fold. Under (M) the population target is mean check loss. W-01’s log-concavity inequality is strict for the three simulation laws. The R driver actually conditions on z (shape aσ+3n/2). For collapsed P, redraw z immediately after σ before reuse. Cost is one latent refresh with the existing exact conditional.

   For F simulations use 1.1τ(1−τ)/minᵤ fᵤ(qᵤ), using simulation_laws.R. For real data pilot local-linear quantile regressions on the nearest min(n,ceil(n⁰·⁸)) design points at each i/n, equal weights, levels τ±h, h=min(n⁻¹ᐟ³,τ/2,(1−τ)/2). Set Hᵢ=[q̂ᵢ(τ+h)−q̂ᵢ(τ−h)]/(2h). Use σ̂=1.1τ(1−τ)maxᵢ Hᵢ if spacings are positive and each window expects at least five observations beyond each outer level. Otherwise flag F as unidentified by this pilot and offer P. Inverse density is sparsity. The symmetric quotient bias is h²q‴/6 for smooth quantiles, with additional spatial smoothing bias; edges and sharp changes can hide the worst location. Taking a maximum also amplifies noise. At .99 there are only five expected exceedances even across all 500 observations. Neither inflation nor a same-data plug-in certifies deterministic slack. Even the oracle point-density threshold differs from the finite-interval secant in (A2) and does not establish global (EM). Cost is two small regressions per location.

3. **Pilot implicit preconditioned MALA on the exact mixture conditional.** Draw local variances sⱼ given θ,λ by e02_lib.R’s draw_iw_exact (returns inverse variances). Conditional precision G=W+D′diag(1/sⱼ)D, mean μ=G⁻¹Wr. Apply the midpoint Langevin proposal θ′−θ=−h[(θ′+θ)/2−μ]+√(2h)G⁻¹ᐟ²ξ. It has form μ+r(θ−μ)+√(1−r²)G⁻¹ᐟ²ξ, r=(1−h/2)/(1+h/2). Set h=1, r=1/3. Gaussian stationarity and symmetric cross-covariance rG⁻¹ give detailed balance and MH acceptance one. Compute μ and an independent conditional Gaussian draw by stacked banded QR, reusing the factor. This preserves a genuine gradient drift, moves polynomial directions jointly and needs no ADMM or step adaptation. Discard local variances before ordinary λ|θ, retain ASIS, and use clamp-free z. Medium implementation cost, especially general-order QR. Confirm HQ accepts the augmented implicit move as P-MALA-like. **Guess:** lower ESS/s than independent Gibbs, potentially comparable in order. For frozen G a linear functional has ESS fraction (1−r)/(1+r)=1/2; this does not predict log-λ ESS across Gibbs blocks.

   Comparator: use the existing proximal MH proposal with uncapped W+(λ²/2)D′D and stable stacked QR. E-05’s cap increase alone left ESS(log λ)≈2/20k. ASIS already rescales rough θ with λ. Repeating it gave little gain; the methods note’s separate joint jump also lost ESS/hour.

4. **Initialise θ and λ together and stop on the slowest monitored quantity.** Start four chains at quantile trend-filter MAPs with λ₀=n⁵ᐟ² times {0.01,0.1,1,10}, σₓ=1 for P or the F scale. Hold λ fixed for 200 stochastic warm sweeps, then release. For proximal MH alone adapt log δ by t⁻⁰·⁶(acceptance probability−0.5) during warmup. Double warmup from 1000 until dispersed chains meet rank split R-hat <1.01 on the latest half, freeze adaptation and discard warmup. Check retained draws every 1000 for the same R-hat and bulk/tail ESS ≥400 on log λ, learned log σ, roughness, polynomial coefficients and θ at five evenly spaced indices. Cap each phase at 100k/chain and flag unresolved fits. Extra chains cost CPU; common-mode trapping remains possible.
