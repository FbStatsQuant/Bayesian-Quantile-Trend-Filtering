\section{Additional Simulation Results}
\label{app:simulation_results}

This appendix collects the simulation results omitted from the main text for compactness. It reports mean absolute deviation (MAD) for all three baseline signals and all three performance measures---MSE, MAD, and MCIW---for the smooth signal fit at difference order $k=2$. The organization follows the interior-quantile and extreme-quantile simulation subsections in Section~\ref{sec:simulations}.

\subsection{Individual sampler at interior quantiles}
\label{app:sim:individual}

\subsubsection{MAD for the piecewise constant and varying-smoothness signals}

Tables~\ref{tab:pc_int_mad} and \ref{tab:vs_int_mad} provide the MAD results corresponding to the MSE and MCIW comparisons retained in the main text. The MAD rankings broadly follow the MSE rankings: the horseshoe is strongest on the piecewise constant signal, while the individual sampler improves on the Laplace variant across most configurations and is especially competitive under Beta noise.

\subsubsection{Smooth signal at difference order \texorpdfstring{$k=2$}{k=2}}
\label{app:sim:individual:smooth}

For the smooth signal, the individual sampler attains the lowest MSE and MAD under Gaussian and mixed normal noise at every interior quantile level. Under Beta noise it is best at all levels except $\tau=0.05$, where the Laplace variant is marginally better. Its credible intervals are also the narrowest across all three noise distributions and all reported levels. The complete MSE, MAD, and MCIW results appear in Tables~\ref{tab:s_int_mse}--\ref{tab:s_int_mciw}.

\subsubsection{Smooth signal at difference order \texorpdfstring{$k=2$}{k=2}}
\label{app:sim:joint:smooth}

At the extreme levels the Onizuka variants are more competitive on the smooth signal than at the interior levels. The horseshoe has the lowest MSE under Gaussian and mixed normal noise, with modest margins over the Joint Sequential Sampler, while both Onizuka variants lead under Beta noise. The Joint Sequential Sampler nevertheless improves on the Laplace variant at every mixed-normal level. Its intervals are much narrower under all three noise distributions, reflecting the sentinel-induced geometric confinement discussed in Section~\ref{sec:sim:joint}. Complete results appear in Tables~\ref{tab:s_ext_mse}--\ref{tab:s_ext_mciw}.
