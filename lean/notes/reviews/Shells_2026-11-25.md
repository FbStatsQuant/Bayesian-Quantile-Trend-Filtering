## Mathematical and conventions audit

- **Cauchy–Schwarz and Tonelli.** Lean factors the integrand by Knight's identity, proves joint measurability, then applies the nonnegative form of Hölder with exponents 2 and 2 on the product of the data law restricted to the score event and the prior measure restricted to the shell. The first square integral is at most the deterministic score bound times the event probability, which is at most one. Tonelli reverses the integrations in the second square integral, where the score event is dropped by monotonicity before applying the remainder bound. This gives the same estimate as the paper's pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz followed by Jensen. [Shells.lean:475](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Shells.lean:475)
- **Remainder and volume.** `s0` implies both `|δᵢ| ≤ L` and `|δᵢ| ≤ min(L, 3fl/(4fu))`. Coordinatewise nonnegativity and the probability laws give integrability for the exponential remainders. The finite product bound is `exp(−(fl/2)‖δ‖₂²)`, at most `exp(−(fl/2)a x)` on the shell, where `x = n εₙ²`. The containing ball contributes `Vᵣ(2a x)^(r/2)`. `Cfine` arises in the score estimate. [CheckIntegrals.lean:37](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Asym/CheckIntegrals.lean:37), [Shells.lean:550](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Shells.lean:550)
- **Score, integrability, and constants.** The projection fixes the polynomial component, and the residual is evaluated using `u − u*`. The score integral enlarges the shell to a set with `‖c − c̄(u)‖₂ ≤ √(2a x)` and uses the coordinate volume formula. The coarse term is at most `(fl/8)a x`. The Laplace computation splits at zero and checks `s + λ > 0` and `s − λ < 0`. The finite one-dimensional integrals give Bochner integrability before the product identity. With `p = 2r − 1`, `D = fl² M / 256`, and `K = 2^p cF(r)² / D^p`, the proof uses `x^(p+1) = n` and `Cfine/(c² M^p) = 4K/c²`. It bounds `8‖t‖₂²/λ² ≤ 2(Cfine/(c² M^p))x` and `|⟨t,u*⟩| ≤ x`. Taking the two square roots gives exactly the exponent in the frozen statement. [Shells.lean:101](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Shells.lean:101), [Shells.lean:202](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Shells.lean:202), [Shells.lean:392](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Shells.lean:392), [Laplace.lean:23](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Asym/Laplace.lean:23), [Laplace.lean:98](/home/fb31/.t3/worktrees/Bayesian-Quantile-Trend-Filtering/review-solW1-5082fc3/lean/BQTF/Asym/Laplace.lean:98)

These give `r ≤ floor(2Dx) ≤ n` and `J = floor(2Dx) − r ≥ Dx ≥ 1`, so natural subtraction agrees with the paper and `1 ≤ J ≤ n − r`.
