\section{Additional simulation results}
\label{app:simulation_results}

This appendix supplements the interior-quantile comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:sim:individual} and the joint extreme-quantile experiment of Section~\ref{sec:sim:extreme}. All tables use $n=500$ and order $k=2$.

\subsection{Interior quantiles}
\label{app:sim:individual}

This subsection reports the measures omitted from Section~\ref{sec:sim:individual}: MAD and MCIW for the varying-smoothness and smooth signals. All entries are averages over valid replications. MCIW is the average width of the $95\%$ pointwise posterior intervals, and it is not available for the ADMM point estimator.

\subsubsection{Varying smoothness}
\label{app:sim:individual:vs}
The MAD comparison (Table~\ref{tab:vs_int_mad}) agrees with the MSE comparison of Section~\ref{sec:sim:ind:vs}. The intervals of Bayesian-PMALA are narrower than those of both Onizuka samplers at every level and under every noise family (Table~\ref{tab:vs_int_mciw}).

\subsubsection{Smooth signal}
\label{app:sim:individual:smooth}
For the smooth signal, Bayesian-PMALA uses $\lambda\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(1,5\times10^{-4})$, no interweaving step, and $20000$ sweeps of which the first $5000$ are discarded; all other settings are those of Section~\ref{sec:sim:individual}. The MAD comparison (Table~\ref{tab:s_int_mad}) agrees with the MSE comparison of Section~\ref{sec:sim:ind:smooth} (Table~\ref{tab:s_int_mse}): Bayesian-PMALA is more accurate than both Onizuka samplers at $\tau=0.25$, $0.50$, and $0.75$ under every noise family and under Beta noise at $\tau=0.05$, and less accurate than both at the other extreme levels. Its intervals are narrower than those of both Onizuka samplers at $\tau=0.25$, $0.50$, and $0.75$ and under Beta noise at $\tau=0.05$; at the other extreme levels they lie between the Laplace and horseshoe widths (Table~\ref{tab:s_int_mciw}).

\subsection{Joint estimation of extreme upper quantiles}
\label{app:sim:joint_extreme}

Tables~\ref{tab:vs_ext_upper_cross_nb}--\ref{tab:s_ext_upper_cross_all} count the crossings among the fitted curves of Section~\ref{sec:sim:extreme}. For two reported levels $\tau_a<\tau_b$, a crossing is an index $i$ at which $\widehat{\theta}_{\tau_a,i}>\widehat{\theta}_{\tau_b,i}$. The neighboring count of a level adds its crossings with the adjacent reported levels, so the count at $\tau=0.97$ involves $0.96$ and $0.98$; the total count adds its crossings with all three other reported levels in $\{0.96,0.97,0.98,0.99\}$. Each pair of levels contributes at most $n$ crossings, so a count can exceed $n$. Entries average the counts over replications. The levels $0.90$ and $0.93$ of the joint fit are excluded. Every retained draw of the joint sampler is ordered at every index, so its posterior means do not cross and its entries are zero.
