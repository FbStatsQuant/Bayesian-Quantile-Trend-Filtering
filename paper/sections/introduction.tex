\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Time-varying conditional quantiles describe how the lower tail, centre and upper tail of a response evolve along an ordered domain. Quantile trend filtering estimates them by combining the check loss of \citet{koenker1978regression} with an $\ell_1$ penalty on discrete differences, which adapts a piecewise polynomial fit to local changes in the signal \citep{tibshirani2014adaptive,madrid2022risk}. \citet{brantley2020baseline} developed it for baseline drift estimation, with non-crossing constraints, extended-BIC selection of the penalty and an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) algorithm; the optimizer returns point estimates at a selected penalty.

We develop a Bayesian counterpart. Under an asymmetric Laplace working likelihood and a global Laplace prior on trend differences, the conditional posterior mode at fixed penalty $\lambda$ and scale $\sigma$ is the quantile trend filtering solution with penalty $\lambda\sigma$, and we infer $\lambda$ and $\sigma$ jointly with the trend instead of selecting the penalty. \citet{onizuka2024fast} also pair the asymmetric Laplace likelihood with trend filtering, using horseshoe shrinkage on each difference; their Gibbs sampler draws the trend from a Gaussian with a changing precision matrix, which is costly for long series. Calibrated loss-based alternatives include \citet{fasiolo2021fast}.

Proximal Markov chain Monte Carlo is designed for nonsmooth posteriors. The proximal Metropolis-adjusted Langevin algorithm (P-MALA) of \citet{pereyra2016proximal} proposes a Langevin step on the Moreau--Yosida envelope of the negative log-density, whose gradient is a proximal map, and corrects with the exact density \citep[see also][]{durmus2018efficient,heng2023bayesian}. In quantile trend filtering the likelihood is nonsmooth as well as the prior: the check loss has no curvature, combined with the difference penalty it has no routinely computable proximal map, and smoothing it changes the target.

We remove this obstacle with the normal--exponential representation of the asymmetric Laplace distribution \citep{kozumi2011gibbs}. Given one latent variable per observation the likelihood is Gaussian in the trend, so the negative log full conditional of the trend is a weighted quadratic plus the $\ell_1$ difference penalty. Its proximal map is a weighted trend filtering problem, solved by ADMM with banded factorizations, and P-MALA applies in its exact form to the whole conditional: nothing is smoothed, and the chain targets the stated posterior. The augmented precision also supplies the curvature that preconditions the proposal. The resulting Gibbs sampler, Bayesian-PMALA, adds conjugate updates for the latent variables, $\lambda$ and $\sigma$, and an interweaving step \citep{yu2011center} for $\lambda$; its cost per sweep grows linearly with the sample size.

For several quantile levels we impose non-crossing on the posterior. Given its neighbours, the trend at each level has its single-level conditional restricted to a box; P-MALA applies unchanged with a box-constrained proximal map, computed by banded interior-point steps when a bound binds, and every retained draw is ordered.

In simulations on the varying-smoothness signal of \citet{onizuka2024fast} and the smooth signal of \citet{brantley2020baseline}, each competitor excels on the benchmark that suits it, the horseshoe on the former and ADMM on the latter, while Bayesian-PMALA is competitive on both and returns posterior draws of the trend, the penalty and the scale. At extreme upper quantiles the joint sampler is the most accurate method on the varying-smoothness signal in most configurations and second to ADMM on the smooth signal, except under mixed-normal noise at the most extreme levels, where it leads; its fits never cross. In the peak-detection design of \citet{brantley2020baseline}, Bayesian-PMALA performs close to ADMM and clearly outperforms the horseshoe sampler, and for long series it is orders of magnitude faster per sweep than the supplied horseshoe implementation.
