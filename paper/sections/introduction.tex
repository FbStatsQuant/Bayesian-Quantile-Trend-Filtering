\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Quantile trend filtering estimates a conditional quantile that varies along an ordered domain, such as time. Consider observations
\begin{equation}
    y_i = \theta_i + \varepsilon_i, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n,
    \label{eq:intro_model}
\end{equation}
with independent errors, where for a level $\tau \in (0,1)$ the trend $\theta_i$ is the $\tau$-quantile of $y_i$. The estimator of $\theta = (\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_n)$ minimizes
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i=1}^n \rho_\tau(y_i - \theta_i) + \lambda \norm{D^{(k+1)}\theta}_1,
    \label{eq:intro_qtf}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_\tau$ is the check loss of \citet{koenker1978regression} and $D^{(k+1)}$ is the difference operator of order $k+1$. The $\ell_1$ penalty on the differences adapts a piecewise polynomial fit of degree $k$ to local changes in the signal \citep{tibshirani2014adaptive,madrid2022risk}. Here $\lambda$, which we call the penalty, sets the smoothness of the fit and must be chosen. Optimization and posterior sampling have both been proposed for this problem; we describe them next.

Quantile trend filtering was developed by \citet{brantley2020baseline} for baseline drift estimation. They solve \eqref{eq:intro_qtf} by the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), impose non-crossing constraints when several levels are fitted, and select $\lambda$ by an extended Bayesian information criterion. \citet{madrid2022risk} established risk bounds for the estimator. The method, however, returns a point estimate at the selected penalty.

Bayesian methods instead place a prior on the differences and replace the check loss by an asymmetric Laplace working likelihood; see also \citet{fasiolo2021fast} for a calibrated loss-based alternative. \citet{onizuka2024fast} use horseshoe shrinkage on each difference. Their Gibbs sampler draws the trend from a Gaussian distribution whose precision matrix changes at every sweep; in their implementation this draw is costly for long series.

Proximal Markov chain Monte Carlo is designed for nonsmooth posteriors. The proximal Metropolis-adjusted Langevin algorithm (P-MALA) of \citet{pereyra2016proximal} proposes a Langevin step on the Moreau--Yosida envelope of the negative log-density, whose gradient is a proximal map, and corrects with the exact density; see also \citet{durmus2018efficient} and \citet{heng2023bayesian}. In quantile trend filtering, however, the likelihood is nonsmooth as well as the prior: the check loss combined with the difference penalty has no routinely computable proximal map, and smoothing the check loss changes the target.

This article develops a Bayesian counterpart of the ADMM quantile trend filtering estimator in which the penalty and the likelihood scale are estimated with the trend. We take an asymmetric Laplace working likelihood with scale $\sigma$ and a Laplace prior on the differences with penalty $\lambda$, and we place priors on $\lambda$ and $\sigma$. At fixed $(\lambda, \sigma)$, the conditional posterior mode is the solution of \eqref{eq:intro_qtf} with $\lambda$ replaced by the effective penalty $\lambda\sigma$. The contributions are threefold. First, we give a Gibbs sampler for a single quantile level, which we call Bayesian-PMALA. Under the normal--exponential representation of the asymmetric Laplace distribution \citep{kozumi2011gibbs}, the negative log full conditional of the trend is a weighted quadratic plus the $\ell_1$ difference penalty. Its proximal map is a weighted trend filtering problem, so P-MALA applies to the whole conditional without smoothing, and the cost per sweep grows linearly with $n$. Second, for several quantile levels we give a joint sampler whose draws never cross: each trend is drawn from its single-level conditional, restricted to lie between the trends at the adjacent levels. Third, we establish posterior contraction for fixed $\lambda$ and $\sigma$. With $\lambda$ of order $n^{(2k+1)/2}$, the posterior contracts around the true quantiles at the rate $n^{-(2k+1)/(2k+2)}$, and the same rate holds at every level of the joint posterior under the ordering constraint. The data are not assumed to follow the asymmetric Laplace distribution; the result requires two conditions on their distribution functions near the quantile, adapted from \citet{madrid2022risk}, and no moment condition. In simulations at quantile levels from $0.05$ to $0.95$, the mean squared error of Bayesian-PMALA is within a factor of $1.22$ of that of the horseshoe sampler on a signal with varying smoothness, and ADMM has the smallest error on a smooth signal.

Section~\ref{sec:methodology} presents the model and the two samplers; computational details are in Appendix~\ref{app:pmala}. Section~\ref{sec:theory} states the assumptions and the contraction theorems for one level and for several ordered levels; the proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, and Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions} analyzes the assumptions. Section~\ref{sec:numerical} reports simulations and two data applications, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} concludes.

\paragraph{Notation.} For a vector $v \in \R^n$, write $\norm{v}_1 = \sum_i |v_i|$, $\norm{v}_2 = (\sum_i v_i^2)^{1/2}$, and $\norm{v}_\infty = \max_i |v_i|$. The first difference operator is $D^{(1)}v = (v_2 - v_1, \ldots, v_n - v_{n-1})$, and $D^{(r)} = D^{(1)}D^{(r-1)}$ for $r \geq 2$; throughout, $D = D^{(k+1)} \in \R^{m \times n}$ with $m = n - k - 1$. The check function is $\rho_\tau(u) = u\{\tau - \mathbbm{1}(u < 0)\}$. For positive sequences $c_n$ and $d_n$, we write $c_n \lesssim d_n$ if $c_n \leq C d_n$ eventually for a constant $C > 0$, and $c_n \asymp d_n$ if both $c_n \lesssim d_n$ and $d_n \lesssim c_n$. We write $\mathrm{Gamma}(a, b)$ for the Gamma distribution with shape $a$ and rate $b$, and $\mathrm{IG}(a, b)$ for the distribution of the reciprocal of a $\mathrm{Gamma}(a, b)$ variable.
