\section{Posterior contraction}\label{sec:theory}

This section studies the posterior of Section~\ref{sec:methodology} when the penalty $\lambda$ and the scale $\sigma$ are held fixed. Section~\ref{sec:assumptions} describes the setting and states two assumptions on the distribution of the data. Section~\ref{sec:main-result} presents the posterior contraction theorem and discusses its rate and the role of the working likelihood. Section~\ref{sec:theory-remarks} relates the fixed $\lambda$ and $\sigma$ of the theorem to the priors used by the sampler. All proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Setting and assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions}

Fix a quantile level $\tau\in(0,1)$ and an order $k\ge1$. We observe independent $y_1,\dots,y_n$ with distribution functions $F_{y_1},\dots,F_{y_n}$, and $\thetas_i$ denotes the $\tau$-quantile of $y_i$; the vector $\thetas=(\thetas_1,\dots,\thetas_n)$ is the target. The observations need not be identically distributed, and the asymmetric Laplace density \eqref{eq:ald} is used as a working likelihood only: the data are not assumed to follow it. For $\theta,v\in\R^n$, let
\[
\TV(\theta)=n^{k}\norm{D\theta}_1,\qquad V^{*}=\TV(\thetas),\qquad
\Delta_n^2(v)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\min\{|v_i|,v_i^2\}.
\]
The factor $n^k$ is the usual scaling of the differences of order $k+1$ on an equally spaced grid: if $\thetas_i=Q(i/n)$ for a function $Q$ on $[0,1]$ whose $k$th derivative has bounded variation, then $V^{*}$ remains bounded as $n$ grows. The loss $\Delta_n^2$ is that of \citet{madrid2022risk}; it is quadratic in the small errors and linear in the large ones.

In the results of this section $\lambda$ is deterministic and $\sigma$ is fixed. We take $\sigma=1$ for simplicity; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} extends to any fixed $\sigma$. Under the working likelihood \eqref{eq:ald} and the prior \eqref{eq:prior_theta}, the posterior of $\theta$ is, for every Borel set $B\subset\R^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:post-fixed}
\Pi_\lambda(B\mid \by)=\frac{\int_B\exp\{-\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_\tau(y_i-\theta_i)-\lambda\norm{D\theta}_1\}\,d\theta}
{\int_{\R^n}\exp\{-\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_\tau(y_i-\theta_i)-\lambda\norm{D\theta}_1\}\,d\theta}.
\end{equation}
This is the conditional posterior of $\theta$ given $(\lambda,\sigma)$ in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}, at $\sigma=1$. It is proper, although the prior is flat on the polynomials of degree at most $k$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). We write $\lamtv=\lambda/n^{k}$ for the penalty on the scale of $\TV$, so that $\lambda\norm{D\theta}_1=\lamtv\TV(\theta)$, and we state the order of the penalty both for $\lambda$ and for $\lamtv$.

We can now state the two assumptions, both on the distribution functions near the target quantiles. They are adapted from \citet{madrid2022risk}: Assumption~\ref{ass:A} is their condition, and Assumption~\ref{ass:B} is the corresponding upper bound.

\begin{assumption}[Lower bound near the quantile]\label{ass:A}
There exist constants $L>0$ and $\fl>0$ such that for any $n$, any $\delta\in\R^n$ with $\|\delta\|_\infty\le L$ and all $i$,
\[
\bigl|F_{y_i}(\thetas_i+\delta_i)-F_{y_i}(\thetas_i)\bigr|\ge\fl\,|\delta_i|.
\]
\end{assumption}

This condition requires each distribution function to grow at least linearly within distance $L$ of its $\tau$-quantile. It holds when $y_i$ has a density bounded below by $\fl$ on $[\thetas_i-L,\thetas_i+L]$, but no density is required.

\begin{assumption}[Upper bound near the quantile]\label{ass:B}
There exists a constant $\fu<\infty$ such that, for the same $L$, any $n$, any $\delta\in\R^n$ with $\|\delta\|_\infty\le L$ and all $i$,
\[
\bigl|F_{y_i}(\thetas_i+\delta_i)-F_{y_i}(\thetas_i)\bigr|\le\fu\,|\delta_i|.
\]
\end{assumption}

This second condition is the matching upper bound, satisfied when the density of $y_i$ is bounded above by $\fu$ on the same interval. It excludes an atom at the quantile, so $F_{y_i}(\thetas_i)=\tau$. Neither assumption involves moments of $y_i$, so heavy-tailed noise such as Cauchy noise is allowed.

The contraction result also requires a bound on the signal: there is a constant $\Vb$ such that $V^{*}\le\Vb$ for all $n$. Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions} analyzes the assumptions and verifies them for the noise distributions and signals of Section~\ref{sec:numerical}.

\subsection{Main result}\label{sec:main-result}

The main theorem states that, with $\lambda$ of order $n^{(2k+1)/2}$, the posterior concentrates around $\thetas$ at the rate $n^{-(2k+1)/(2k+2)}$ in the loss $\Delta_n^2$. We write $\E_{\thetas}$ for expectation under the true distribution of $\by$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $y_1,\dots,y_n$ be independent and satisfy Assumptions~\ref{ass:A} and~\ref{ass:B}, and let $V^{*}\le\Vb$ for all $n$. Let $\sigma=1$ and
\[
\lambda=c\,n^{(2k+1)/2},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\lamtv=c\,n^{1/2},
\]
with a constant $c>0$. Then there exist constants $M$, $C>0$ and $n_0$, depending only on $c$, $k$, $L$, $\fl$, $\fu$, and $\Vb$, such that for all $n\ge n_0$
\[
\E_{\thetas}\,\Pi_\lambda\bigl(\theta:\ \Delta_n^2(\theta-\thetas)>M\,n^{-(2k+1)/(2k+2)}\bigm|\by\bigr)\le C\,n^{-1/(2k+2)}.
\]
The bound is uniform over all laws of $\by$ that satisfy Assumptions~\ref{ass:A} and~\ref{ass:B} with the given constants and $V^{*}\le\Vb$. The same holds for any fixed $\sigma=\sigma_0>0$, with constants depending also on $\sigma_0$.
\end{theorem}

Posterior draws lie within $M\,n^{-(2k+1)/(2k+2)}$ of $\thetas$ in the loss $\Delta_n^2$, apart from posterior mass that vanishes in expectation; the posterior therefore contracts at the rate $n^{-(2k+1)/(2k+2)}$, with no logarithmic factor. For the default $k=2$, $\lambda=c\,n^{5/2}$ and the rate is $n^{-5/6}$.

The proof, in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, writes the posterior as a ratio and treats its two terms separately. The denominator is bounded below through the prior mass of a ball centered at a locally smoothed version of $\thetas$. The numerator is bounded above on shells around $\thetas$. A localization step first confines the posterior to a set on which $\|\theta-\thetas\|_\infty\to0$, so that Assumptions~\ref{ass:A} and~\ref{ass:B} apply to every coordinate. The localization step is where $k\ge1$ is needed.

\begin{remark}[Comparison with the rate of the estimator]
\citet{madrid2022risk} obtained the rate $n^{-(2k+2)/(2k+3)}$, up to a logarithmic factor, for the quantile trend filtering estimator. The rate of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is slower. Heuristically, the posterior averages over windows of order $\lambda^{1/(k+1)}$ points, so its spread in the loss $\Delta_n^2$ is of order $\lambda^{-1/(k+1)}$, while its smoothing bias is of order
\[
V^{*2}\lambda^{(2k+1)/(k+1)}n^{-(2k+1)}.
\]
The two balance at $\lambda\asymp n^{(2k+1)/2}$, which gives $n^{-(2k+1)/(2k+2)}$, the exponent of the lower bound for linear estimators over this class \citetext{\citealp{donoho1998minimax}; \citealp[Section~5.1]{tibshirani2014adaptive}}. The posterior mode is the quantile trend filtering estimator with effective penalty $\sigma_0\lamtv$. At $\lamtv=c\,(n\log n)^{1/(2k+3)}$, the localization argument of Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} shows that every mode attains $\Delta_n^2\le C\,n^{-(2k+2)/(2k+3)}(\log n)^{1/(2k+3)}$ with probability at least $1-C(n\log n)^{-1/(2k+3)}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Misspecification]
Every probability statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is under the true distribution of the data, which need not be asymmetric Laplace. The posterior is therefore a Gibbs posterior, or quasi-posterior, built on the check loss, in which $1/\sigma$ acts as a learning rate \citep{syring2023gibbs,atchade2017contraction}. It concentrates on the true $\tau$-quantile because, whatever the distribution of $y_i$, the $\tau$-quantile minimizes the expected check loss and hence the Kullback--Leibler divergence from the truth to the asymmetric Laplace family \citep{sriram2013posterior}. Misspecification affects the spread of the posterior but leaves its center at the true quantile. The theorem therefore describes where the posterior concentrates and how fast; it makes no statement about the frequentist coverage of its credible sets \citep{yang2016posterior}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The penalty and the scale in practice}\label{sec:theory-remarks}

The contraction result holds for a deterministic $\lambda$ of a given order and a fixed $\sigma$, whereas the sampler of Section~\ref{sec:methodology} places the priors \eqref{eq:hyperpriors} on both and updates them with the trend. We describe how the two settings relate.

For the penalty, the full conditional \eqref{eq:lambdaupdate} is $\lambda\mid\theta,\by\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_0+m,\ b_0+\norm{D\theta}_1)$, with mean $(a_0+m)/(b_0+\norm{D\theta}_1)$. Under a weak prior, the roughness $\norm{D\theta}_1$ of the current trend sets the penalty: $\lambda$ is large when the trend is smooth and small when it is rough. The rate $b_0$ enters only through the sum $b_0+\norm{D\theta}_1$, so it matters only when $\norm{D\theta}_1$ is of the order of $b_0$ or smaller, that is, for trends that are nearly polynomials of degree $k$. Without it, the conditional mean of $\lambda$ would be unbounded for such trends.

For the scale, once the latent variables are integrated out, $\sigma\mid\theta,\by\sim\mathrm{IG}(n+a_\sigma,\ S(\theta)+b_\sigma)$ with $S(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_\tau(y_i-\theta_i)$. Hence $\sigma$ concentrates near $S(\theta)/n$. This is close to the average check loss $\bar\sigma_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\E\,\rho_\tau(y_i-\thetas_i)$ when that average is finite and bounded away from zero, which excludes Cauchy noise. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with $\sigma_0=\bar\sigma_n$ describes the posterior at that scale.

The posterior with random $\lambda$ and $\sigma$ is not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. We sample both in practice so that no penalty has to be selected by cross-validation or by an information criterion.
