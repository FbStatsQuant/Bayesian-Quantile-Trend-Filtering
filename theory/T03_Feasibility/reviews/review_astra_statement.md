# External advisor review of STATEMENT_DRAFT.md (verbatim)

**Verdict.** Keep the true quantile vector, Padilla's Δ²ₙ, and expected posterior tail probability. They give Stage A a meaningful target even for Cauchy errors. I would pursue n^{-3/4}, up to logs, as a **candidate upper bound at one deterministic tuning**. The first obstacle is L8, even before the heavy-tail question in L9. The proposed sieve contains enough directions to fit the signs of the residuals, which defeats its uniform empirical-loss bound even under Gaussian errors. This objection concerns the proof strategy. The contraction rate itself remains open.

**1. Fix the sieve argument first.** In fitted-value space, A_ε contains the entire empirical-ℓ₂ ball of radius ε in the complement of the quadratics. Adding the free quadratic component allows every perturbation of that size around a constant displacement. Take a zero truth, Gaussian median errors, and a constant displacement of 2√M ε. Moving each fitted value by ε toward its observation stays in the sieve and, for large fixed M, stays on the claimed outer shell. It improves average empirical loss by order ε, whereas the constant displacement costs order Mε². Thus L8 fails at the scale where it is needed. A promising replacement is to **integrate** likelihood ratios over the small-ball component and control the structured enlargements separately. Any such argument needs an explicit bound on the integrated numerator outside the sieve. Convexity along rays alone gives no bound on its volume.

L7 also uses an infinite measure: the complement has infinite full-prior mass whenever its rough-coordinate complement has positive probability. State concentration under the normalized difference prior, account for centering at Dθ*, and integrate the three flat coefficients in both numerator and denominator using the same normalization. Posterior propriety itself is straightforward because the ALD likelihood is integrable in θ. L3 still needs an actual Laplace small-deviation bound. A′ removes the particular L8 obstruction but inherits the outside-sieve problem.

**2. Assumptions and benchmark.** Keep A1 and a uniform lower margin. A2's upper half rules out an atom at the quantile and supports the quadratic evidence bound. Define population excess risk as the expectation of the **loss difference**. Its absolute value before expectation is bounded by the coordinate displacement, so this part genuinely needs no moments. Monotonicity extends the lower margin to a global linear-tail bound, and Lipschitzness extends the local quadratic *upper* bound to a global quadratic upper bound with a larger constant. This helps L6 use an empirical-ℓ₂ ball. In A3 the sup-norm bound can be dropped by quadratic equivariance and bounded variation of the remainder on the unit grid. In A4 the endpoints express sufficient tuning targets at most. An upper bound that fails to vanish cannot establish necessity.

For fixed V*>0 and σ, both optimizations are algebraically correct: n^{9/4} gives n^{-3/4}, and n^{7/3} gives n^{-2/3} for A′. The shift term is a prior-mass cost. Its tightness as posterior bias is unknown. The displayed fixed-σ theorem also cannot identify a power of σ. A2's corollary needs the published tuning: on the raw difference scale Padilla's Theorem 3 uses λσ = c n^{15/7}(log n)^{1/7}, with a sufficiently large confidence-dependent constant and V* = Θ(1). Its MAP upper bound is n^{-6/7}(log n)^{1/7}. A3's squared-error implication is correct if L10 holds in true probability.

**3. Decisions on the six doubts.**
1. Use the ALD runs to test the spread interpretation. A faster observed slope is compatible with the upper bound. A slower slope merits checking total Δ²ₙ error at the target tuning before switching to A′.
2. Keep the moment-free objective. A bounded-coordinate likelihood-ratio argument at sufficiently small ω is a reasonable first step. It still needs a separate posterior tail bound for large coordinates. Neither log-concavity nor sub-exponential errors alone supplies L9.
3. Report the two upper bounds. Claiming the posterior *pays* n^{3/28} requires a posterior lower bound.
4. Omit the knot-count rate. The number of knots alone does not control total variation without a bound on jump magnitudes.
5. Treat b₀ ≍ n^{-2} as a Stage-B candidate. The Gamma conditional gives a necessary scale b₀ ≲ n^{-5/4} for appreciable mass at n^{9/4}. The stronger calibration and the fixed-b₀ rate rely on an unproved marginal balance.
6. Start with a fixed positive class radius and uniformity over laws sharing the margin constants. Leave growing radii and the full tuning window for later. At the oracle tuning an unspecified finite log power is harmless. In the proposed full window, even the lower endpoint requires C₀ > 3c for the displayed rate to vanish.
