# Referee audit of Theorem H

**Verdict — ACCEPT WITH FIXES.** The localization rate, joint TV bound, and learned-scale extension hold. Section 6.3 needs a change to prove the advertised hyperprior-independent event. The limitations asserted in §§5 and 9 are too strong.

1. **CONFIRMED — Margin, translations, and cones (§§1–3).** Integrating the lower secant gives the stated local margin. Convex radial extension from ±L gives the global factor f/2 even when the error has no first moment, since each loss increment is bounded by its argument in absolute value. The two defining inequalities are strict at zero and continuous. At the first feasible radial scale at least one binds. Assigning ties to the Huber face leaves exactly the TV equality on the other face, where the radial scale depends only on u. The supporting bound h_L(x) ≥ L|x|−L²/2 gives the stated section radius. On the two coordinate sets, the squared and absolute sums give the advertised average absolute error by Cauchy–Schwarz. Also min(x²,|x|) ≤ 2h_L(x)/min(1,L) coordinatewise. All these arguments allow large coordinates.

2. **CONFIRMED — End trimming and leverage (§4.1–2).** There are at least three retained knots. For interior j the indicated three consecutive knots bracket j, so Lemma F's interpolation error is at most √8.1 times h^{5/2}. The left and right end constants are bounded by √(2⁵/20) and √((7/3)⁵/20), respectively, both below 2.85. On each proposed cell the zero third differences extend the quadratic through its last index. The final cell has at least h+3 points. Enlarging the space to independent cell quadratics and applying L10-I gives diagonal leverage ≤9/h. Trimming assures the bound for all remainders. For m divisible by h the untrimmed space contains s_m=e_n, with leverage one, violating 9/h when h>9. Other remainders need not require trimming, and a knot merely near the endpoint need not itself give a coordinate vector.

3. **CONFIRMED — Width and Markov (§§4.3–5).** The projection trace term is ≤2nr/√h. The maximum coordinate of the projected noise has expectation ≤3√(2 log(2n)/h) by independence and Hoeffding. The residual columns pair with the full TV budget Tn/Λ₋. After flooring x, the three constants in units of √log(2n) nr/√x sum to at most 2√2+12+2.85√2 < 18.9. Correct the intermediate upper bound 4.03 to 4.031, since 2.85√2 > 4.03. The absolute-value contraction and symmetrization factor is four. The resulting 8·4·18.9=604.8 is covered by 605. Markov then costs 1/K. Substituting K=(M_n/C_H)^{7/12} gives exactly the stated power 7/2 in the floor condition. For this proof, explicitly carry K_n→∞ and the window condition into every asymptotic restatement, including §§7–8.

4. **CONFIRMED — Cone integrals (§6.1–2).** On the Huber face the exponent 3at pays 2a and leaves a Euclidean radial integral of 8π(R_c/a)³. On the TV face, use t_u=n²‖u−u*‖₁/T₀ in the section bound, including an empty section if t_u≤1. Its upper bound Γ/(Tn)+V/T₀ holds at every supported rate. The Gamma moment at 1/2 costs 2^m, and (Tn/2)V/T₀=VΛ₋/(2n²). H4 implies positivity of the tilt and the displayed exponential remainder. Both prefactors after division follow from R_c≤1.5nL.

5. **CONFIRMED — Denominator event requires a repair (§§1, 6.3).** The normalized mixed restriction g depends on p, hence the displayed Ω_D has only been proved for each prescribed p. The following construction proves the stronger statement with the same constants. Write Λ=Λ₋. Take independent U with law Π_Λ conditioned on B_{ε/2} and C uniform on the Euclidean three-ball of radius ε√n/2. For each dataset let F(v)=E_{C,U} ℛ(NC+vP⊥SU). This function is convex and even in v by symmetry of U, hence F(Λ/ℓ)≤F(1) on the window. In the integral at ℓ shift u by u*, paying at most exp(−2V) by the pointwise Laplace density inequality. Translate c to cancel the polynomial projection of the innovation displacement. Represent the unshifted innovation by (Λ/ℓ)U₀ with U₀ distributed as Π_Λ before restriction. Restrict U₀ to B_{ε/2} and the translated c to the above ball, and apply Jensen. The resulting lower bound at every window point is the ball volume times Π_Λ(B_{ε/2}) exp(−2V−ωF(1)). L3 gives Π_Λ(B_{ε/2})≥exp(−nε²−6), since (30·2^{2/5})^{5/6}<21.5. At v=1 orthogonality gives squared empirical norm ≤ε²/2, so independence and the same L2/Jensen calculation give mean ≤C₁nε²/2 and variance ≤nε²/2 for F(1) under sampling. The event F(1)≤(1.25C₁+1)nε² is common to all p and ω, with failure probability ≤0.5/(nε²). Integration over the window proves (3) as printed.

6. **CONFIRMED — Asymptotics and conditioning (§§6.4, 8).** For fixed T the floor assumption forces r=o(1), x→∞, and x=O(n^{2/3}). H4 follows from T/4>log 2. The ratio r²/ε² has powers Λ₋^{1/21} and (log n)^{29/42}; nr² grows at least polynomially, and nε² diverges. Thus −log p₀=o(nr²) absorbs the window penalty in both tails. Positivity alone suffices only for the finite-sample inequality. Under truncation p₀ is exactly the window mass divided by the survival probability including the endpoint. The posterior inequality follows by total probability for any B and proper posterior. For a rate-b exponential law the conditional window mass is 1−exp(−bΛ₋), with the stated sufficient log condition. Other hyperpriors require their own window bound. Carry Lemma S's assumptions into the scale conclusion.

7. **CONFIRMED — General-floor scale containment (§7).** In V2T Step 2(a) the L3 cost divided by n is at most 30/(10⁴)^{2/5}+6/n<1 for n≥100 under H1. Mixing preserves the unit-ball mass ≥e^{−n}. The bound ‖P⊥θ*‖ₙ≤V, the flat-coordinate ball, the scale interval [1,2], and the data event Q*/n≤B+1 then give common evidence ≥e^{−Cn}. In Step 2(b) the data roughness event forces ‖u‖₁≥nd/4 at small loss. Integrating the Laplace Chernoff bound gives ≤2^m exp(−Λ₋nd/8), sufficient against the IG negative moment exp(O(n log n)) and the evidence whenever Λ₋/log n→∞. The other two integrals use the flat bound and prior probability one. Both data events are uniform with the common row-average UI modulus and fixed d,B. No window mass enters containment. Disintegration now permits H on the resulting compact, and the Lipschitz loss bound gives Lemma S's hypothesis. This establishes the extension under its additional moment assumption.

8. **CONFIRMED — Sup-norm limitation has an exception (§§1, 9).** Write η=2r²/min(L,1). L10-I's Huber corollary gives vanishing sup norm when η→0 and η²T₀→0. Here the latter expression is of order M_n² n³Λ₋^{−11/7}(log n)^{12/7} for fixed T. At Λ₋=n² it vanishes for sufficiently slowly diverging M_n, for instance log log n. Thus “L10-I gives nothing” misses a genuine part of this window. Conversely C1 does not automatically take over immediately above n². Its sup-norm condition requires Λ₋≫n^{23/11}(log n)^{12/11}, with its other hypotheses still applicable.

9. **CONFIRMED — A diverging envelope does not rule out Bousquet (§§5, 9).** Even outside the sup-norm consistency regime, L10-I controls the envelope relative to the concentration threshold. For s=‖δ‖∞>1 its proof gives s≤max(16Cη,(2K₁)^{5/3}η^{2/3}T₀^{1/3}) by splitting the two interpolation terms. Thus the body envelope b is O(1+nr^{4/3}Λ₋^{−1/3}) for fixed T,L. With K fixed and Λ₋≫(log n)³, b log n/(nr²)→0, since Λ₋r²≫(log n)³. The weak variance is at most 2nr²(1+b/L) by the Huber split. For the centered class with both signs, the envelope is at most 2b. Bousquet at log n therefore adds O(√((1+b)nr² log n)+b log n)=o(nr²), using the proved expectation bound. Taking fixed K≥2 leaves slack below fnr²/8. This also yields a fixed sufficiently large multiplier with vanishing failure probability, subject to the corresponding window-mass condition at that radius. The published Markov argument by itself gives the weaker diverging-multiplier statement.
