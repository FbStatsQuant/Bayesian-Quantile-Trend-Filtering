# Independent referee report on Lemma W and Theorem C1

**Lemma W — ACCEPT.** Both displayed width bounds hold, including at the truncation endpoints. The entropy comparison needs a qualification.

**Theorem C1 — REJECT as stated.** The two cone bounds prove the oracle result. The general tuning claim needs a restriction controlling the truth's TV relative to the TV radius, as well as the complete L6 hypothesis. A truth-specific denominator budget, even with a valid L6 center, cannot replace the TV restriction.

## Findings

1. **CONFIRMED — Step 6 assumes an upper restriction on λ.** C1 lines 28–38, 73–78, 92, 109. Condition (i) gives a lower bound on λ. The calculation actually requires, to obtain its displayed exponent,

   \[
   a+1+m\log2+V\lambda/(2n^2)\le Tn/4.
   \]

   Indeed the upper bound before simplification has exponent \(-Tn/2+a+1+m\log2+(Tn/2)s_0\). The Gamma MGF, the coefficient \(Tn/2\), the bound \(t^3\le6e^t\), and the translated section volume are correct. A simple repair is to add \(V\lambda=o(n^3)\) to (i)–(ii), including in the headline. Then the displayed inequality holds with slack for every fixed \(T\ge4\), since \(a=o(n)\) uniformly on the compact interval. At \(\lambda\asymp n^{5/2}/V\), \(s_0\asymp n^{-1/2}/T\), with equality for the exact oracle tuning.

2. **CONFIRMED — The missing restriction can change the answer.** C1 lines 29–38, 71–76. Here is a counterexample under the pointwise budget in (ii), even strengthened by the L6 center condition. Fix \(V>0\), \(T=4\), \(\omega=1\), \(\lambda=n^{7/2}\), and \(\theta_i^*=(-1)^i V/(8n^3)\). Take independent quantile-centered shifted Gaussian errors with common law. A1–A3 hold with fixed constants and \(TV(\theta^*)=mV/n\to V\), while \(T_0=4/\sqrt n\). Take \(u_h=0\) and \(\varepsilon^2=n^{-19/20}\). The projected approximation error is at most \(\|\theta^*\|_n=O(n^{-3})\). L3 gives a bounded negative log small-ball probability since \(n^{5/2}/(\lambda\varepsilon)=n^{-21/40}\). Also \(n\varepsilon^2=n^{1/20}\ll nr^2\asymp n^{1/7}(\log n)^{6/7}\), and (i) holds.

   To check the posterior directly, condition on \(c,u_{-j},y\). Relative to its value at \(u_j=0\), the log likelihood changes by at most \(B|u_j|\) where \(B=\max_j\sum_i|S_{ij}|=O(n^3)\) by the cumulative-sum formula (Theorem B lines 15–20). Integrating the two exponential bounds against the Laplace density gives \(E(|u_j|\mid c,u_{-j},y)\le(\lambda+B)/(\lambda-B)^2\) for large \(n\). Consequently \(E[TV(\theta)\mid y]=O(n^3/\lambda)=o(1)\) for every dataset. Markov and the triangle inequality give \(\Pi(TV(\theta-\theta^*)>T_0\mid y)\to1\) uniformly in the data. This refutes even the fixed-ω assertion under the pointwise reading. If (ii) is intended as a simultaneous tuning certificate for the entire A1–A3 class, the example establishes failure for a single admissible truth, without asserting that this tuning has such a certificate for all truths. State the uniform quantifiers and impose the explicit TV restriction of finding 1 to justify Step 6.

3. **CONFIRMED — Supply both L6 inputs and the uniformity quantifiers.** C1 lines 30, 36–38, 80, 110; Theorem B lines 134–144, 200–206, 228–233. Require a common deterministic sequence ε and constant \(C_{\rm pr}\) such that for each admissible truth there exists a deterministic \(u_h\) with both the stated prior mass and \(\|P_\perp S(u_h-u^*)\|_n\le\varepsilon/2\). The printed budget alone permits a center arbitrarily far from the truth. Theorem B's oracle construction supplies both properties uniformly. In particular its window \(w\asymp n(\varepsilon/V)^{2/5}\) diverges with \(w/n\to0\), \(\lambda\varepsilon\asymp n^{25/12}(\log n)^{1/12}\) for fixed V, and joins J5–J8 pay the spread and quadratic shift at the half radius. No Theorem B posterior conclusion or global MGF assumption is used.

4. **CONFIRMED — Choose a valid deviation level.** C1 lines 54–57, 108. On the signed centered class with coordinate envelope \(b=2s_K\), variance sum bound \(v=nr^2\), Bousquet gives \(G\le EG+\sqrt{2x(v+2bEG)}+bx/3\) except on a set of probability at most \(e^{-x}\). Independence across observations suffices and compactness and continuity give a countable dense indexing set. The formula in line 108 is correct. The little-o claim with \(x=\log n\) needs \(nr^2\gg\log n\), whereas (ii)'s radius comparison gives only \(nr^2\to\infty\). For example at zero truth take \(\lambda=n^4(\log n)^3/(\log\log n)^{7/2}\), giving \(nr^2\asymp\log\log n\); (ii) holds with \(n\varepsilon^2=\sqrt{\log\log n}\) by L3. Use \(x=\sqrt{nr^2}\) under the original radius comparison. Alternatively, finding 1's upper restriction directly implies \(nr^2\gg\log n\), so the printed level works in the repaired theorem.

5. **CONFIRMED — Use the correct endpoint in the polynomial prefactor.** C1 lines 82–88. In line 84 replace ω by \(\omega_1\) in **both** \(\omega^{-3}\) and the negative exponent, and by \(\omega_2\) in the positive denominator exponent. The instruction “ω₂ elsewhere” misses the prefactor. The first term vanishes since \(\varepsilon nr=\sqrt{n\varepsilon^2}\sqrt{nr^2}\to\infty\). For the second, \(r=o(1)\), \(1/\varepsilon\le\sqrt n\) eventually, and \(n\varepsilon^2=o(n)\). The L6 event bounds the averaged excess loss before multiplying by ω (L6 proof lines 53–69). Thus one intersection controls every ω on each dataset. Take the supremum there, bound by one on the complement, and use the union bound. For measurability, the two endpoint integrands sum to an integrable dominator by L5 propriety, giving continuity of the ratio in ω and a countable dense supremum.

6. **CONFIRMED — Qualify the width comparison and clarify rounding.** W lines 15–19, 39–41; C1 lines 55, 107. The entropy comparison for the full body must include the null-space term of order \(\sqrt n\rho\): constant vectors alone give this order for Rademachers as \(T_0\downarrow0\). For the actual lemma put \(q=(T_0\sqrt n/\rho)^{1/3}\). If \(q<1\), take \(k=1\); the tail before its logarithm is at most \(\sqrt n\rho\sqrt q\). For \(1\le q\le m\), \(q\le k\le2q\); for \(q>m\), the actual tail is zero and \(\sqrt m\le\sqrt q\). Use the capped choice in C1. The exact condition \(q\le m\) there is \(\lambda r\ge T n^{7/2}/m^3\), and it is unnecessary for the bound.

## Verified calculations

- W lines 23–37. Orthogonality and \(\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon)\preceq I\) give head factors \(\sqrt3,\sqrt k\). For each tail column Hoeffding gives variance proxy its squared Euclidean norm, at most \(91.2^2 n^5/(5k^5)\) by the integral bound on the sixth-power tail. The signed maximum uses \(2m\) MGFs and needs no coordinate independence within the transformed vector. Multiplication by \(\sqrt n T_0/n^2\) gives the stated coefficient \(91.2/\sqrt5<40.8\). W lines 45–54 use independent ghost observations and independent Rademacher signs. Conditional on the data the check-loss maps vanish at zero and are 1-Lipschitz. Absolute-value contraction costs at most 2, symmetry of the linear body removes the absolute value, and symmetrization costs 2. The centered coordinate variance is bounded by its uncentered second moment. No data moments enter.
- C1 lines 22–29, 44–62. Balancing the nonparametric width against \(nr^2\) gives \(r^{7/6}\gtrsim n^{-5/12}T_0^{1/6}\sqrt{\log n}\), hence exactly the displayed powers after substituting \(T_0=Tn^3/\lambda\). The width-to-target ratio scales as \(C_r^{-7/12}\), and the additive head ratio vanishes by (ii). Squaring the formula for \(r^2\) and multiplying by \(T_0\) gives the threshold \(n^{23/11}(\log n)^{12/11}\). At the oracle the squared-radius power is \(-4/7\), and the L6 squared-radius power is \(-5/6\). L10-I lines 9–13 give precisely 6 and 7.06; (i) gives both \(r=o(1)\) and \((r^4T_0)^{1/5}=o(1)\). The lower secant bound enters only via integration to the local quadratic margin \(f\delta_i^2/2\); its appearance in probability thresholds just spends that margin. L6 uses the upper half alone.
- Both faces are Borel, cover the exterior, assign ties to the norm face, and positive homogeneity puts the rescaled vector in the body. Convexity applied to \(\delta/t=t^{-1}\delta+(1-t^{-1})0\) works for either sign of the excess loss. C1 lines 63–72 use \(N^T\delta=c-\bar c(u)\); translation has unit Jacobian. Polar integration gives \(\int e^{-h\|z\|}dz=8\pi/h^3\), with \(h=a/(2\sqrt n r)\). This yields exactly the factors 8 in line 68 and 16 after division by the L6 flat volume in line 84. On the TV face the section is contained in a translated ball of volume \(V_3(\sqrt nrt)^3\). With findings 1, 3 and 5, these estimates prove the claimed compact-uniform oracle conclusion.
