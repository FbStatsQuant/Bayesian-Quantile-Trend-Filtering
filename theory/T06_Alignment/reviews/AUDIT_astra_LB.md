# Independent referee report on Theorem LB

**Verdict: ACCEPT WITH FIXES.** The finite-sample inequality in §8.1 holds as written, including its constants, for a β-admissible truth and tuning. At the oracle the lower bound determines the polynomial exponent when paired with an applicable upper theorem. Several statements outside §8.1 need narrower quantifiers. The following checks use the repository's audited inputs and hand inequalities.

1. **CONFIRMED — innovation blocks (§2).** Write T = 1 + E + ⋯ + E^{h−1}. The averaged difference is T(E^h−1)^3/h = T⁴(E−1)^3/h. Thus its innovation indices run from i_k to i_k+4h−4, the last at most 4Kh−3 ≤ n−3. Successive starting indices differ by 4h. This proves independence even for h=1 and for any choice of right inverse S. Four translates of the length-h stencil give squared coordinate coefficient norm 20/h. The nonnegative innovation weights sum to h³ on 4h−3 sites, giving squared norm ≥ h⁶/(4h−3). Multiplication by the Laplace variance 2/λ² gives the stated variance.

2. **CONFIRMED — density and translations (§3).** The reference density with height G and slope parameter 2G has the same half-line mass as g. Concavity of the log ratio makes its nonnegative set an interval containing zero, so the signed second moment of g minus the reference is nonpositive after subtracting the crossing radius squared. The endpoint cases give equality almost everywhere. Convolution preserves log-concavity and symmetry, hence the density bound λ/h^{5/2} holds everywhere. Integrating the resulting product bound over a translated g-dimensional ball gives the stated volume bound with no centre dependence.

3. **CONFIRMED — both small-ball bounds (§4).** In the Euclidean case K ≥ n/(8h) gives base at most √(320πe)rλ/h^{5/2} ≤ e⁻¹. In the Huber case 29.1·168^{−6/5} ≤ 1/16 gives nr² ≤ n/(16h) ≤ K/2. Even for odd K one may retain g = ⌈K/2⌉ clean blocks. Their squared block error sums to at most 20nr²/h. Paying 2^K ≤ 4^g gives base at most 4√(640πe)/805 ≤ e⁻¹. The ceiling bounds on h yield the denominators 116.8 and 465.6, safely rounded to 117 and 466. A feasible null-space coefficient is eliminated by L_k(Nc)=0 for every c. The covering union is measurable and its bound is uniform for each deterministic centre. Integrating its indicator times the maximal c-section volume V₃ max(√n r,nr²)³ handles the improper flat factor.

4. **CONFIRMED — log-free L3 (§5).** Lemma F provides the same column bound for every j at each J. The projected approximation has rank at most J because P_J P⊥ = P_J−P_N. The squared Frobenius residual therefore bounds the sum of covariance eigenvalues past J by 29.1² n⁴ J^{−5} ΣV_j. A single event of probability ≥ 1/2 bounds ΣV_j by 4m/λ² simultaneously for all ranks. For q≥2, J=⌊q/2⌋ gives the factor 2·3⁵=486, and the trace bound covers q=1. Thus b=1944·29.1² n⁵/λ² suffices in L3 Step 6. For rigorous rounding write (3110.4·846.81)^{1/5} < 19.24 (raise to the fifth power), rather than an equality. The cost is at most log(8/3)+3.85+3.85·19.24 x^{2/5}, below 5+75x^{2/5}. This argument works also when the Gaussian series cutoff exceeds m.

5. **CONFIRMED — expectation and the centre (§§6,10).** Quantile optimality gives E X_i≥0 even at an atom. At each fixed displacement the loss increment occupies an interval of length |δ_i|. Hoeffding on small coordinates and the pointwise Lipschitz bound on large ones give c_ω=max(ω²/8,ω) after multiplication under independence. Tonelli applies to the nonnegative integrand under the σ-finite prior, and restriction to the deterministic truth ball permits a pointwise bound by exp(c_ω nr²). Technically Tonelli also applies to a jointly measurable random-ball indicator. In that case the indicator stays inside the expectation. The triangle inclusion in §10 is valid and gives only its stated localized estimator conclusion.

6. **CONFIRMED — denominator and shift costs (§7).** L6 uses a deterministic centre within ε_d/2 and the shifted ball of radius ε_d/2. The unshifted cost is at most 75·2^{2/5}D+5 ≤ 99D+5. The quadratic truth has zero charge. For (A3), L4 charges at most λV/n², giving β=Vλ^{4/3}/n³. With the floor bandwidth in (R2), L4q-M gives squared approximation error ≤ ε_d²/4. The half-radius L4q hypothesis is met at λε_d ≥ 102 n^{10/7}(log m)^{1/7}, which implies the column-increment condition. The charge is ≤ 6.288·4^{1/5} C_M^{11/5} λ²V^{12/5}n^{−5}ε_d^{−2/5}. Dividing by D gives the stated power (Vλ/n^{5/2})^{12/5}. At the oracle w grows at least on the order of n^{5/6} for fixed V, and w/n tends to zero throughout the high-tuning route. Its lower endpoint grows more slowly than the n^{9/4} endpoint of L4, giving the asserted overlap.

7. **CONFIRMED — theorem constants and tuning restrictions (§§0,8,11).** At r²=c₀λ^{−1/3}, x^{2/5}/466=2ΣD. The lower endpoint λ^{1/3}≥32Σ makes n/16≥2ΣD, and the upper endpoint gives D≥42 and the requisite block-fit inequality. Both Huber radius conditions follow from c₀=(932Σ)^{−5} and λ≥1. The c-section radius is ≤D. Dividing by L6's deterministic evidence floor on its good event gives 8D^{3/2} and exponential cost 5−(Σ+1)D, with failure probability ≤1.44/D. Independence of the two bounds is unnecessary. Use a common upper bound on β to choose a single c₀ in uniform limits. The overview should state the eventual lower restriction on λ and D→∞. Above the oracle §§8.3 and 11 must retain both tuning restrictions even when using the growing (R2) budget. For example λ=n^{59/20}, V fixed, gives β of order n^{27/25} by that route, whereas λ^{1/3}=n^{59/60}; the lower endpoint fails. The theorem does not certify that extrapolation with that budget.

8. **CONFIRMED — oracle interpretation (§8.4).** The oracle lower radius follows from λ≤a₂n^{5/2}/V. The upper result under (EM) is Theorem B. For an arbitrary fixed scale with full (A2) and the oracle truth/tuning conditions, cite the fixed-scale pieces C*-3 and C*-4 of FABLE_A_n56 on a compact interval containing that scale. The named Theorem C* itself concerns a learned scale and includes the scale-containment assumptions. The two-sided assertion certifies exponent 5/6 in the Huber loss with the displayed upper logarithm. Under (A1) and upper (A2) alone only the lower conclusion has been established here.

9. **CONFIRMED — compact scales and learned scale (§§9.1–9.2).** L6's event concerns the prior-average excess loss and works simultaneously in ω. Convexity bounds each numerator by the sum at the two endpoints and doubles only the exponential term. For continuity one may use uncentred likelihoods dominated on the compact interval by the integrable likelihood at ω₁. Disintegration against the posterior scale and the independent compact-containment result of S §2 justify the transfer. Explicitly require bounded β, λ→∞ and D→∞ for this asymptotic use (or the eventual finite-window conditions with D→∞), in addition to the stated scale assumptions. A bounded truth can establish scale containment, but alone supplies no bounded shift budget. Even for a quadratic truth λ/log n→∞ alone allows λ=n⁴, outside LB's window.

10. **CONFIRMED — capped mixture and constant-rate truncated exponential (§9.3).** Monotonicity of both applicability conditions and of the small-ball exponent in ℓ proves the integrated numerator bound; a mixed posterior probability also needs a mixed denominator. For constant b₀ the prior tail at Λ₋+t equals exp(−b₀t). S's flat integral and IG negative moment cost exp(O(n log n)), and V2T Step 2 gives evidence ≥ exp(−Cn) with probability tending to one. This proves vanishing posterior tail also for t=C′n log n/b₀ with sufficiently large C′. On the compact scale interval, use the two endpoint numerators, the common event of V2T (2), and window mass 1−exp(−b₀Λ₋)→1. With c=c₁(log n)^{−5/6}, the numerator exponent has order D₋(log n)^{1/6}=o(n), so the n/16 truncation is inactive. The block-fit and radius restrictions hold eventually. A sufficiently small c₁ covers the displayed denominator cost and the polynomial volume ratio, since D₋ is of order n^{1/6}. State the inherited V2T hypotheses, including the common UI modulus and positive scale lower bound. The scaled hyperprior lacks this floor-tail argument.

11. **CONFIRMED — the log-free V0T lower bound can also be closed (§9.3, item 3).** For fixed κ₋,V take ε²=Λ₋^{−1/3}, D₋=nε². Use the §7 mollified centre at that ε for all ℓ in [Λ₋,2Λ₋] (zero for V=0). The half-radius hypotheses hold eventually at the floor and hence on the window. L3⁺ costs ≤99D₋+5 throughout, while the shift costs ≤4 C_b(Vκ₋)^{12/5}D₋ by the upper endpoint. The deterministic normalized mixture on V2T's good set obeys its same mean and variance bounds. Thus on an event of failure probability ≤1.25/D₋ its evidence is at least V₃(√D₋/2)³ p₀,ₙ exp(−5−KD₋), with K=99+4C_b(Vκ₋)^{12/5}+ω₂(1.25C₁+1). Take a fixed 0<c<min(1,2^{−1/3}/168) so small that 2^{−2/5}c^{−1/5}/466>K+c_{ω₂}+1. For r²=cΛ₋^{−1/3} the block-fit and n/16 restrictions hold eventually because Λ₋^{1/3}=o(n) and D₋=o(n). The endpoint numerator ratio is then O(D₋^{3/2}e^{−D₋}), and the scale and tuning tails vanish by item 10. This establishes the proposed improvement for constant b₀ and fixed V,κ₋.

12. **PLAUSIBLE — ancillary sketches (§§8.5,11).** The formal optimum in §8.5 is a=(99/(5A))^{5/6}, A=ω(1.44C₁+1), and its cost is 6(99/5)^{5/6}A^{1/6}. The resulting σ scalings are Euclidean-ball calculations and require the radius and tuning restrictions to be rechecked if ω varies. For the log-free (EM) upper substitution, J7's shift now consumes a fixed multiple of nε² depending on C and a₂, so enlarge C_pr accordingly. For the arbitrary-scale upper sketch, a diverging Markov threshold and control of the largest fine score are still required. Keep the sketches distinct from §8.1. Finally, §11's proposed numerical interval [1/117,75] is not the lemma's universal enclosure: the lower bound has a minimum, and dividing the upper bound by x^{2/5} adds 5/x^{2/5}.
