## Findings

1. **CONFIRMED — F's interior and endpoint estimates.** The weights on three consecutive grid knots reproduce the quadratic in the knot index. For $i\ge\kappa+2h+1$ every truncated column equals that quadratic, including its two zero values at offsets 1 and 2. For $i\le\kappa+2$ all columns vanish. On the remaining indices the absolute error is bounded by $9/4$ times the largest column. Summing its squared values gives at most $(9/4)^2(2h)^5/20=8.1h^5$. At the left end subtract the full quadratic, leaving the reversed binomial tail through $t=j+1$. At the right end take zero, leaving the forward tail through $t=n-j-1$. Each squared norm is bounded by $(h+1)^5/20$ in its respective end case. For fewer than three grid knots, the smaller of these two general distance bounds is at most $((m+5)/2)^5/20\le(3.5h)^5/20$. Thus 2.85 and 5.13 round upward, as do 16.2 and 29.1 after conversion. The condition $J\ge4,m\ge9$ indeed implies $3h\le m$. **Fix** the opening binomial typo to “zero at $t=0,1$.” Second differences are piecewise constant. To justify the 2.85 remarks also for small $J'$, $h=1$ gives zero residual, and for $h\ge2$ in Case 4, $(3h+4)/2\le2.5h$ already suffices. The unused unequal-gap remark also follows directly. On the first interval at distance $x$ from the first knot only the last weight can be negative, so the absolute-weight sum is $1+2x(g_1-x)/(g_2(g_1+g_2))$, with the reflected formula on the second interval.

2. **CONFIRMED — W′ and the scope of the C1 substitution.** The head uses the rank $J+3$, and the tail uses Hoeffding on the signed columns with variance proxy at most $c_F^2h^5$. At $q=(T_0/\rho)^{1/3}$ the two powers balance. Below one the tail scales as $q^3\le\sqrt q$, and above $m$ the tail vanishes. In C1 this requires $r^2\gtrsim n^{-6/7}T_0^{2/7}(\log n)^{6/7}$, with the head requiring $nr^2\gtrsim1$. Substitution gives $r^4T_0\asymp n^3\lambda^{-11/7}(\log n)^{12/7}$ for fixed $T$. **Fix** the summary of the general window by carrying forward $V\lambda=o(n^3)$ and C1(ii) with $n\varepsilon^2=o(nr^2)$ for the *new* radius. At the oracle the prerequisite holds: the two squared radii have powers $-5/6$ and $-5/7$. C* also follows using the original C1 radius.

3. **CONFIRMED — specify the deterministic domain of Tonelli's bound.** Let $D_n=n\varepsilon^2$, $s_0=\min(L,3f/(4\omega_2\bar f))$. **Fix** by defining $E_a=\{aD_n<\|\delta\|_2^2\le2aD_n,\ \|\delta\|_\infty\le s_0\}$ before §3.1. The two Knight formulas give nonnegative remainders on both half-lines with the stated strict-indicator convention. Continuity at the quantile gives mean-zero score. Integration on either side gives $ER_i^2\le\bar f|\delta_i|^3/3$, while $ER_i=m_i\ge f\delta_i^2/2$. The choice of $s_0$ gives $2\omega^2 ER_i^2\le\omega m_i$. 4. **CONFIRMED — coarse event.** For $d=J+3$, $EQ\le d/4$. Mixed diagonal/off-diagonal covariances vanish by centering. The diagonal variances have coefficient at most $1/4$, and the ordered off-diagonal sum has coefficient at most $2(1/4)^2$. Thus $\mathrm{Var}(Q)\le d/4$ by the projector identity. Chebyshev yields $4(J+3)/(9(J-1)^2)\to0$. At $J=\lfloor f^2MD_n/128\rfloor$, the coarse score is at most $\sqrt{2aD_nJ}\le faD_n/8$ on every shell. No adjustment required.

5. **CONFIRMED — fine factor.** With $b_J=C_F(n/J)^{5/2}\sqrt{2\log(2m)}$, the range-one Hoeffding bound gives failure at most $(2m)^{-3}$ for the maximum score. Integrating out the three flat coordinates first permits extending the remaining positive integrand over the product Laplace law. Uniformly up to the top endpoint, $2\omega_2 b_J/\lambda=O(n^{-5/12}(\log n)^{1/12})\to0$. Hence the exact product of $(1-4\omega^2t_j^2/\lambda^2)^{-1}$ is finite, and half its log is at most $4\omega^2 m b_J^2/\lambda^2$. Using $V^2n=D_n^6/(C^6\log n)$, $J\ge f^2MD_n/256$ and $\log(2m)\le2\log n$ gives exactly the displayed upper constant $C_{\rm fine}$ times $\omega^2D_n/M^5$. The absolute truth shift after taking the square root is bounded by $\omega_2 b_JV/n^2=O(\sqrt{D_n})=o(D_n)$ for fixed $M$. The claimed rate uses this bound with its logarithm already included.

6. **CONFIRMED — shell coverage and one event.** **Fix** the unspecified terminal index. Writing $x=r^2/(M\varepsilon^2)>1$, the minimal index is $\lceil\log_2 x\rceil-1$. Both $\lfloor\log_2 x\rfloor$ and C*'s ceiling suffice, because the upper edge of shell $k$ is $2^{k+1}M\varepsilon^2$. The Opus report correctly identified the missing choice, but its warning that a floor can miss the top shell is incorrect. For $x\le1$ the body has no bad shell. Choose $0<\eta<f/8$. Intersect both score events, C1's $\Omega_G$, L6's $\Omega_6$, and all endpoint Markov events at all selected shells. The additional remainder failure probability is at most $2\sum_k e^{-2\eta\omega_1 2^kMD_n}=o(1)$. On that same intersection convexity bounds each numerator by the sum of its endpoint values. L6 holds there at every scale with the common oracle prior budget from B, joins J5–J8. Dividing gives volume ratio $(8a)^{3/2}$. If $\omega_1(f/8-\eta)M>\omega_2(1.44C_1+1)+C_{\rm pr}+C_{\rm fine}\omega_2^2/M^5$, the first shell has a fixed positive exponential margin and subsequent margins grow geometrically in the shell index. The deterministic summed bound vanishes. On the complement the posterior supremum is bounded by one. Continuity by endpoint domination gives measurability. All probability bounds use common class constants, proving the supremum-inside-expectation claim.
