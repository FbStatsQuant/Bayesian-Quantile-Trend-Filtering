# Independent referee report on Theorem J

**Verdict — ACCEPT WITH FIXES.** The four numbered conclusions in §2.2 follow for fixed interior levels under the stated product construction and the audited single-level hypotheses. The corrections concern the scope of the extensions and the interpretation of the finite-sample condition.

1. **CONFIRMED — Conditioning and nuisance parameters (§1).** Fix the data and write μ for the product of the proper level-specific posterior measures. Multiplying the working kernels gives μ after normalization because their integration variables are disjoint. The trend marginals have positive density on open balls, so a product of sufficiently small balls about strictly increasing constant curves gives μ(C)>0 for every data vector. This also covers deterministic oracle tuning, using a point mass for each tuning parameter. Integration gives Π_C(B)=μ(B∩C)/μ(C). Conditional on the trends in C, the nuisance kernel is the product of the single-level conditional kernels, almost everywhere. Shared data create no sampling independence across levels, and none is needed. This verifies the stipulated composite with its level-specific priors. Matching any other prior specification to the paper requires a separate identification. Delete the categorical assertion in §5 ruling out every possible Bayesian representation.

2. **CONFIRMED — Separation at every design index (§2.1).** For any true quantile x at level τ the quantile inequalities give F_i(x−)≤τ≤F_i(x). Taking negative increments tending to zero in upper (A2) gives F_i(x−)=F_i(x), hence F_i(x)=τ. Write h for the adjacent quantile gap. If h≤L, evaluation at the two quantiles gives Δτ≤f̄h. If h>L, the claimed lower bound already holds. Take the minimum neighborhood radius and maximum upper constant over the fixed levels. The argument works within each individual F_i, including i=1,n and heteroscedastic designs. It makes no assertion between observed locations. Correct the distance statement to **at least** g₀/2. The exact distance in the full-array sup norm is half the smallest actual gap, attained by moving an adjacent minimizing pair to its midpoint.

3. **CONFIRMED — Uniform inputs and transferred rates (§2.2).** The joint law class is a subset of each single-level class. Specify the common row-average uniform-integrability modulus over laws at each level, along with its fixed constants. The learned-scale and mixed-tuning sup-norm conclusion is already quantified as a supremum over laws in FABLE_A_n56 §3.1 with the Astra audit corrections. For C* use §2 there with a fixed positive oracle variation bound at each level. C1 alone supplies compact-uniform localization. The sharper radius follows by intersecting the TV body with the improved squared empirical-norm ball and applying L10-I. With TV radius of order √n this gives s_j of order n^(−7/30)(log n)^(1/15). Each level may use its own scale interval, tuning law or deterministic oracle, and admissible threshold. A fixed finite maximum of the thresholds suffices. The rate n^(−5/6)(log n)^(1/6) is for **squared empirical norm**. Scale consistency holds at every fixed relative tolerance.

4. **CONFIRMED — Event bookkeeping and factor two (§2.3).** Write U_j for exceeding the stated sup-norm radius. Whenever adjacent radii sum to at most the corresponding minimum true gap, Cᶜ is contained in the union of U_j. Equality at the tube edge is allowed. Thus μ(Cᶜ)≤Σ_j Π_j(U_j). The random event μ(Cᶜ)>1/2 concerns the data, and Markov bounds its sampling probability by twice the expected crossing mass. On the complementary data event the conditioning ratio costs at most two, proving the displayed bound. In fact the exact TV identity, with TV defined as the supremum over events, gives Π_C(B)≤μ(B)+μ(Cᶜ) for each data vector. For a union of level events this yields the same bound with coefficient **one** before taking expectations. Restrict the sentence after the quantitative display to the three choices of bad event used in Step 4. The inequality applies to arbitrary level events, whose posterior probabilities may be large.

5. **CONFIRMED — Moving grids (§0 and §3(d–e)).** Keep the PROVED label for fixed levels. Even with fixed q, letting the levels move with n uses a triangular array of quantile indices. The gap inequality still guarantees tube inclusion, but the cited contraction theorem is stated for fixed τ. Pointwise convergence at each fixed τ does not give convergence along a moving sequence. Mark this extension conditional on verifying the marginal bounds along the sequence. For growing q the sum of marginal errors must vanish. Grid spacing alone gives only a packing restriction, with the log factor retained if claiming the precise order.

6. **CONFIRMED — Finite-sample interpretation (§§0, 4–5).** The condition 2 max_j s_j≤min(L,Δτ/f̄) **suffices**. Its failure gives no lower bound on crossing probability. Actual gaps may exceed this lower bound and actual errors may be smaller than the tube radii. The onset also depends on the single-level bounds. On the gap-controlled branch, the displayed radius gives the scaling n/(log n)^(2/7) of order (2c f̄/Δτ)^(30/7) at the nominal threshold. Neither the constants nor a crossing estimate at the paper’s sample sizes is supplied. Remove assertions that the constraint must be active there or that the threshold is astronomical for every c. Independent pairing of level-specific posterior draws conditional on the same dataset can estimate μ(Cᶜ) there. A finite-sample estimate cannot establish an asymptotic regime.

7. **PLAUSIBLE — Small-gap route (§4), conditional only.** A high-probability ordering-mass lower bound exp(−C_C nε²) suffices if the bad-event exponent exceeds its cost, with all bounds on a common high-probability data event. This is one route. Multiple inputs remain open, including the exponential scale-tail bound for each desired tolerance, the evidence upper bound, ordered centers with controlled shift costs, and the shifted tube probabilities including the flat directions. A larger trend threshold M alone cannot improve a scale event at a fixed tolerance. The proposed small-ball estimate needs a quantified log power: at fixed q, λ of order n^(5/2) and a of order ε, the power term is n^(1/6)(log n)^(−1/30), so a multiplier (log n)^b fits the target budget nε² only for b≤1/5. The fallback balance a^(−1/2)n^(1/20) of order na² gives the stated squared-radius power n^(−19/25), ignoring logs. Its intersection and shift estimates remain unproved. Present both rates in this regime as conditional calculations.
