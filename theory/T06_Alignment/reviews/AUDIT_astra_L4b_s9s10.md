# Independent referee report on L4b §§9–10

**Verdict — ACCEPT WITH FIXES.** The shift identity, pointwise variance lemma, deletion inequality and typical-class mean estimate hold. The rectangular truncation proof of Proposition SS has a substantive gap. The body sup-norm estimate it imports requires a smallness condition that fails inside the proposed window. Findings 7–8 give a replacement proof using the body's total excess mass. This proves the stated **fixed-pair** SS conclusion under strict (ML) and $\beta<6$. In fact the same proof reaches the audited mixture floor $C n^{26/17}\operatorname{polylog}(n)$ under these margins. It gives an evidence bound without resolving the posterior hypothesis (XE). Throughout, take the small constants to fit both strict margins, and impose the earlier audit's H-sharp floor $\ell/[K^6(\log n)^{27/2}]\to\infty$, $\ell\le n^2$. On these polynomial windows $K$ may grow slowly.

1. **CONFIRMED — exact shift (§10.1).** For the truth-centred comparison prior set $B=D^TH_V^{-1}D$. Completing the square with pseudo-data $s+\psi/f'$ changes the log integrand at fixed $V$ by exactly $\omega s^T(I-B)\psi-a s^TBs/2$. Thus the ratio is at most $\exp(a s^T\mu_V)$, even for signed shifts. The flat subspace contributes to the linear term through $I-B$. The determinant cancels in the ratio. Specify that centring the original Laplace prior is a one-sided comparison costing $\exp(\ell\|D\theta^*\|_1)$ once, before taking the mixture. Its log is $O(\ell/n^2)=o(k)$ here.

2. **CONFIRMED — PV and choice of width (§10.2).** Complete the two squares in the variational formula for $a(aI+D^TV^{-1}D)^{-1}$ using $e_i=c-D^Tq$. This gives $G_{ii}\le\|c\|^2+a q^TVq$. The row of the local quadratic projector has squared norm equal to its diagonal leverage, at most $9/w'$, hence one-norm at most 3. Subtract the quadratic interpolating the first three nodes of the window. The remaining Newton coefficient at every internal stencil is bounded by $(w')^2/2$, so pairing with $c-e_i$ costs at most $2(w')^2$ per stencil. This proves the advertised $9/w'+4a(w')^4\sum V_j$, including at either data boundary. Sum only over stencils wholly inside the window. For one or two nodes take $c=e_i$. For block sums the two partial end blocks add at most $4w$ indices. Constants 17 and 13 are the continuous-width evaluations. Use absolute constants for integer, feasible widths. In row 3 put $t=2^i$ and take $w'=\max(4w,\lceil(t\varrho)^{-1/6}\rceil)$. Since $t\epsilon<1$ and $\epsilon=110\gamma^{-1}\varrho w^6$, this is within a constant of the desired width on the audited window. Thus $G_{ii}\lesssim t^{1/6}\kappa/n$. In maximum-only classes use the single-coordinate bound $G_{ii}\le1$ when the proposed width is below three. The upper cap $n$ is immaterial here since $\kappa\gg1$.

3. **CONFIRMED — deletion (§9.3).** Disintegrate in affine coordinates $y=A\delta$ and $z=\delta-y\in\ker A_{\rm lin}$. Interpolation fixes $y$ and scales $z$ by $1-\lambda$, including for oblique projections. Convexity and change of variables give the displayed Gamma MGF bound. At $\lambda=1/2$ the probability bound is $(2/e)^d e^{-t/2}$. The conditional proof fixes the exterior first. The soft-cutoff determinant follows from concavity of $\log(1-\lambda x)$ on $[0,1]$. D2 uses $A=I-P$. In D1, $F_W\ge\rho_W$ gives the loss-only conclusion. For the amplitude conclusion use B1's anchoring boundary conditions (three fixed nodes on a side suffice), $F_W-\rho_W\ge N-2\rho_W$ and the stated loss Lipschitz bound. These give $\ell N/2\le2\ell\rho_W+2g+t$. Keep the boundary qualification when using the Newton estimate.

   If “dominated by Gamma” means ordinary stochastic order, add the following argument for projections. The MGF comparison alone establishes only exponential-moment order. For $d>0$, fix $y$ and the direction of $z$ in the fibre. Conditional radial density is proportional to $r^{d-1}e^{-g(r)}$, where $g(r)=\Phi(y+r u)-\Phi(y)$ is convex and $g(0)=0$. On the positive range of $g$, its inverse radius $r(v)$ is increasing and concave, and $r(v)/v$ decreases since $g(r)/r$ increases. The positive-part density divided by the Gamma density on $v>0$ is proportional to $(r(v)/v)^{d-1}r'(v)$, a decreasing function a.e. Conditional on $g>0$ this density ratio gives stochastic domination by single crossing. Mass at $g\le0$ only decreases the positive tails. Mixing over fibres and directions preserves the bound. The finite coercive posterior potential here ensures radial integrability. For $d=0$ the decrement vanishes. The soft-cutoff claim only needs its displayed MGF sense.

4. **CONFIRMED — (XE) suffices for LO-low (§9.1).** Huber energy gives $L\|v\|_1\le2Mk'$. In the interval-score bound take the displayed $\eta_1$ to obtain $\eta k+o(k)$. LK from earlier findings 2 and 8 controls both error terms on this polynomial window. To state a literal $o(k)$ score choose an additional vanishing multiplier, for example $1/\log n$, in $\eta_1$; the polynomial LK slack covers the extra logarithm. Clipping gives $h_L(x)=x^2/2-v(x)^2/2$. Intersect (XE)'s good event with the body to get posterior mass at least one half. Charge at most another $\omega\eta k$ for excess energy, then shrink the proof slack to obtain the prescribed LO coefficient. Replace “(XS) ⇐ (XE)” in §9.5 by “(XE) suffices for the evidence comparison.” The score estimate controls the signed linear functional, while literal (XS) contains $\|v\|_1$. For the near-$L$ sufficient sup bound use the count at $L$ and the body. If the sup event has mass exactly $3/4$, its intersection with the body still suffices for halving, while the resulting bound on (XE) failure by itself is $1/4+o(1)$.

5. **CONFIRMED — narrow the obstruction (§9.2).** The count at $L/2$ is at most $16Mk'/L^2$. Each completed excursion consumes at least $(1-\theta)L$ variation, with the endpoint allowance in the draft. The resulting sign-labelled interval entropy is $o(k)$ even with $1-\theta=1/\log n$. Round the width in T1 upwards and include its $O(n/w+1)$ template multiplicity from earlier finding 11. Its exponential dominates this factor here. For the deterministic shape estimate count blocks with large **actual** internal-plus-left stencil mass of $D\delta$ directly. Each stencil has multiplicity at most two for $g\ge3$. Use right anchors or account separately for an end block. The count on minimal boundary cost $\rho_I$ alone bounds a smaller quantity and cannot establish the pathwise estimate.

   A stronger arithmetic check changes the interpretation of (e). On this entire polynomial window $k'/h=O(n\ell^{-2/3}(\log n)^{1/6})=o(1)$. Thus even **one** cluster of width comparable to $h$ is impossible on the body. Delete the proposed constant-width band in units of $h$. For shorter excursions the count times a uniform squared excess bound still loses the indicated logarithmic factor. (XE) permits isolated larger excesses. The template decrement is a sufficient width test, and at a width with positive net coefficient a larger removable amplitude actually increases the decrement. The roughness cost of a smooth height-$L$ bump of width $h$ is of order $L/h^2$. The fraction of $n/h$ such bumps affordable under $Tn/\ell$ is of order $Th^3/(L\ell)=T/(\omega L)$ **from roughness alone**. These calculations diagnose the failure of the particular count argument. They give neither a converse to T1 nor a necessity theorem for (XE).

6. **CONFIRMED — typical mean (§10.3).** On $\Omega^{(2)}\cap\{\max V_j\le c_0v_1\}$ use the row-2 upper envelope even if the sharp lower law holds. Its trace including the three flat modes is $O(\kappa)$ by earlier findings 4–5. On the corresponding fixed-matrix score event $\psi^TG_V\psi\le C(\kappa+\log n)$. PV and Cauchy–Schwarz then give $\|\mu_V\|_\infty\le C\kappa/(f'\sqrt n)$ simultaneously over that class. It is the upper bound that vanishes iff $\ell\gg n^{3/2}$ at fixed scale. The smallness of the mean itself has only been established in the sufficient direction.

7. **CONFIRMED — the rectangular truncation has an invalid input (§§10.4–10.5).** The advertised $B_n$ uses the small-Huber branch of L10-I. Its hypothesis includes small $\eta_H^2T_0$, whose available upper estimate has order $n^3\ell^{-5/3}(\log n)^{1/3}$ for fixed body constants. At $\ell=n^{8/5}$ this grows polynomially. Outside that branch, writing $b=\|\delta\|_\infty>1$ gives $\|\delta\|_n^2\le b\eta_H$, hence the general interpolation inequality yields only $b\lesssim\eta_H+\eta_H^{2/3}T_0^{1/3}$. The latter term scales as $n\ell^{-5/9}$ up to logs. Substitution of the *claimed* $B_n$ does give $k'\kappa B_n/n\asymp n^{8/5-x}$ up to logs at $\ell=n^x$. It does not justify using that power. Also the large-row-3 conversion $\sqrt{t}\le t\kappa/\sqrt{cn}$ needs its fixed $c^{-1/2}$, and a rectangular normalization would have to retain $(1-e^{-a B_n^2/2})^{-|S|}$ or use the strict margin to absorb it. Delete the “iff” and “binding” assertions.

8. **CONFIRMED — replacement proof of Proposition SS.** Use a deterministic bound on the **sum** of shift magnitudes. Fix $\theta<1$ sufficiently close to one and a finite $b>0$ sufficiently large that
   $$
   c=\int_0^b e^{-a((1-\theta)Lz+z^2/2)}\,dz,\qquad c a(L-m)>1
   $$
   for some fixed $m>0$. This is possible since the limiting product at $m=0$ is $L\sqrt{\pi a/2}>1$. For a cluster coordinate $x=|\delta_i|$ integrate the shift magnitude over $[(x-L)_+,(x-L)_++b]$. At $x>L$ completion of the square leaves the half-Gaussian integral on $[0,b]$. At $\theta L\le x\le L$ the linear coefficient is $L-x\le(1-\theta)L$. In both cases the integral lower bounds $c e^{-a h_L(x)}$. The product box lies in the signed simplex
   $$
   \|s\|_1\le U=2Mk'/L+bN_0=O(k'),\qquad N_0=16Mk'/L^2 .
   $$
   Enlarge to this simplex **before** removing the body restriction. Its definition now depends only on the chosen signed set and deterministic constants. Positivity allows integration over all fits for each fixed shift and then the exact identity of finding 1.

   For $d_S=|S|\le N_0$ and $\mu_c$ a class bound on the mean sup norm, the shift integral divided by $c^{d_S}$ is at most one when $\mu_c\le m$, by integration on the full orthant. On every other class it is at most $(caL)^{-d_S}\exp(a\mu_c U)\le\exp(C k'\mu_c)$ since $caL>1$. In particular there is no rectangular volume penalty. Take the audited $w\asymp\kappa$, $c_0\asymp\kappa^{6/5}$ with the large constants and dyadic rounding of earlier finding 5. On the mixture window through $n^{9/5}$, $k'/\sqrt n=o(1)$ polynomially. The following comparisons retain slack for the original class sums.

   - Typical and row 2 have multiplier at most one by finding 6.
   - Row 1 at level $t=2^i>c_0$ has $\mu_c\lesssim\kappa t^{1/6}/\sqrt n$. This uses PV and the fixed maximum-envelope score bound $O(\kappa t^{1/6})$, using $\|\psi\|^2\le n$ beyond trace saturation. Its extra log cost is $o(\kappa t^{1/6})$ uniformly in $t$. Enlarge the old gain constant by one before choosing $c_0$, and the old dyadic sum is valid.
   - Row 3 has $G_{ii}\lesssim\kappa t^{1/6}/n$ and score quadratic $O(\kappa t^{5/6})$. Thus $\mu_c\lesssim\kappa\sqrt t/\sqrt n$ and the extra log cost is $o(\kappa\sqrt t)$ uniformly. The audited gain $O(\kappa t^{5/6})$ absorbs it at every $t\ge 2$, with the same choice order for $w$.
   - Pool row 4 at the first failed threshold as in earlier finding 5. The maximum envelope at $c_0$ gives extra log cost $o(\kappa c_0^{1/6})$, covered by the existing gain budget against $w/(16\epsilon)$. All score matrices in these estimates already occur in the audited finite-class proof.

   The atypical sums retain their exponential remainder and the typical determinant coefficient is unchanged. Sum over the $e^{o(k)}$ signed sets from finding 5, apply the centred-prior cost once and double the body numerator by posterior halving. With finding 4 this gives the claimed $2e^{4\omega\eta k+o(k)}$ times the audited §3.6 bound. The argument uses no additional posterior hypothesis and only the original mixture compatibility condition. Thus SS may be called proved **with this replacement**. The $8/5$ arithmetic in the draft supplies neither a proof nor a necessary floor. The argument concerns fixed tuning and scale.
