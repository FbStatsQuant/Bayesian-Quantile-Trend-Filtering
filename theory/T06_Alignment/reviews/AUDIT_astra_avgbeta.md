# Referee report on the averaged Occam condition

**ACCEPT WITH FIXES** for the fixed-pair LO upper bound and the U and U-σ upper-window conclusions. The comparison gives the sufficient strict threshold 6 with the stated averaged ratio. Uniform results require a class-uniform positive margin and secant bounds valid on the bodies used. The matching-lower-bound conjecture has a different status.

1. **CONFIRMED — weighted integral (§2, §7.2; LO audit 1).** Put A = DW⁻¹ᐟ² and P = P̃. Then (I−P)W¹ᐟ²A₀ is a right inverse of A with range in (ker A)⊥, hence equals A⁺. Consequently A⁺(A⁺)ᵀ = L⁺. Completing the square and integrating the three flat coordinates gives exactly W1(a), including det(NᵀWN)⁻¹ᐟ². For the unperturbed shrinkage, M(ρL⁺) = (I+L/ρ)⁻¹−P, so the resolvent trace upper bound in W1(d) drops a nonnegative term of size at most 3/w_min. Apply the fixed upper envelope to the *entire* negative square before expanding; apply the lower envelope only to the determinant. Both truth-shift signs in §7.2 are correct.

2. **CONFIRMED — block bracketing (§4).** The row-principal submatrix on internal stencils is block diagonal. Its ordered eigenvalues majorize the corresponding smallest full eigenvalues and are bounded above by those of the direct sum of D_b D_bᵀ/w_b⁻. Decreasing log(1+ρ/x) and discarding the remaining nonnegative terms gives the cost lower bound. Correct the first Remark's explanation: the retained block cost *underestimates* full cost. For gain, deleting the crossing row outer products and lowering W enlarges the inverse. The three null directions of each block contribute exactly 3/w_b⁻, yielding +4 with Lemma 2a. The constants 2π and π/3 survive. Apply LO audit 7 per block: in the displayed gain tail use J_b = floor(η n_b) ≥ 1 in place of η n_b, or choose integer cutoffs from the outset. These cutoffs exist on the window for fixed η and B.

3. **CONFIRMED — BV errors and scale choices (§§4–5, 7.5–7.6).** Write h = ceil(n/B). Summing block oscillations costs at most h TV(f^a) for either power a. Relative to the corresponding full sums this gives the two displayed constants in §7.5 times h/n in place of 1/B. Equality h/n = 1/B requires divisibility. With balanced blocks κ_b ≥ c₋¹ᐟ⁶ k_*^min floor(n/B)/n for either sign, so a sufficiently large fixed lower bound on k_*^min/B suffices. The summed finite-size remainders are O(B(1+log k̄_*)) and O_η(k̄_*⁶/n⁵) for bounded weights; the latter is o(1) on the LO window. For growing B the logarithmic overhead is negligible relative to k̄_* if B log k̄_* = o(k̄_*). At LO's upper endpoint instead fix the desired slack and a positive margin, take B large, then η,γ small, then K₀ large. The fixed-B logarithm belongs in the polynomial prefactor. The leading costs are −π c₋¹ᐟ⁶ k̄_* and at most (π/6) k̄_* β̄ c₊¹ᐟ⁶(1+η)/[(1−η)(1−η²/2)], up to the stated variation and additive errors. This establishes the advertised coefficient from below in magnitude.

4. **CONFIRMED — noncommuting mixture error (§3, §5.2 item 5, §7.5; LO audit 3).** Delete the sketch's Loewner bound M(X+E) ≤ M(X)+E. Even for a rank-one E, B E B can have a different range from E. The valid bound uses B E B, B=(I+X)⁻¹, by the inverse tangent inequality. Cyclicity gives tr(W⁻¹ B E B) ≤ tr(E)/w_min since B W⁻¹ B ≤ I/w_min. Conjugating the original positive error costs at most w_max in trace, yielding precisely Θ for the weighted gain error. The determinant costs w_max tr(E_original)/2 by the log-determinant tangent. These are the two mixture perturbations; the baseline score gain must retain its weighted trace. Using tr 𝒟 ≤ 4w²n⁴/3 in §7.5's width bound pays for both errors for 0<γ<1. It leaves the leading ratio unchanged.

5. **CONFIRMED — local remainder and scores (§7.1–7.3; LO audit 1, 2, 5, 9).** Use the audited shell proof with its common scalar lower bound and additive loss. Integrating the coordinate secant bound from either side of zero gives m_i ≥ f_i δ_i²/2 throughout the body. Retain full (A2) for the inherited cone/upper-slope arguments; only the Gaussian margin needs the improved local slopes. The score matrix acts on the original independent ψ. Its mean quadratic form is t tr Q and its variance is bounded by (ω²/w_min)tr Q/4. The lower bound for tr Q uses DW⁻¹Dᵀ ≤ DDᵀ/w_min and counts min(m,floor(q)−3) modes at shrinkage at least 1/2 for 4≤q≤n. The deterministic shift vector has squared norm at most (ω²/w_min)x² since B²≤B, yielding Hβ′_max/2 after the negative x²/2 penalty. The rank-three flat event costs 3Kβ′_max/2 once.

6. **CONFIRMED — exceptional variances, window, endpoint (§7.4–7.6; LO audit 2–4).** With q=k_*^min≥4, an explicit sufficient C₂ is β′_max(π²/6+4+K/2+1/(2t)). The small-level quadratic gain is at most β′_max[(π²/6)q_i+4], the large-level deterministic gain at most β′_max q_i/(2t), and the shift costs β′_max K2^(i/6)/2. The failure sums are O(Θ/q+1/K). Use η≤1/2 for the Cramér bound. Taking the cutoff and lower width exactly as in §7.4 pays for both exceptional contributions at rate exp(−2π k_*^max). For fixed K the smallest integer width and cutoff are of order q^(6/5)+log(2m). The upper width then requires ℓ/n² ≳ (q^(6/5)+log(2m))^(3/2)/√q. Its power part gives ℓ ≳ n^(99/43); the logarithmic part is smaller uniformly, and the width is o(m). Keep the lower-window constant dependent on K and the fixed proof parameters, as in LO. Use the finite cone inequality in §7.6 with an admissible common slope: Vℓ/(2n²) ≤ n/(2TK) and T/4>log 2. A numerical lower bound on q suffices at this endpoint. No limiting growth of q there is needed.

7. **CONFIRMED — the uniform secant profile and shared flat event (§8).** State (REG) for a deterministic profile valid on *all* frozen panel bodies, allowing dependence on n and P with common positive lower/upper bounds and BV bound. A sufficient choice takes lower secants on the largest deterministic working radius across the panels. Any coordinatewise lower bounds on that interval suffice. Require inf over the asserted class and all sufficiently large n of 6−β̄(ω₂) > 0. Mere strict inequality for each separate member does not furnish a common band constant. For this choice, W = ω(1−η)F with the same F at every node. For each orthonormal flat direction e of range(F¹ᐟ²N), the random variable eᵀF⁻¹ᐟ²ψ is common to every node; only its deterministic multiplier varies. Hoeffding gives the displayed three-direction event. If slopes are instead chosen separately on each frozen panel, the flat projector can change: take a union over panels with failure O(n⁻⁴ log n) and use the worst panel margin and rate. A slope valid only on a smaller node body cannot be used on its enclosing panel. For the designs a concrete common profile is (1−o(1)) times the point-density profile: uniform local continuity of the densities near the quantiles, a positive density minimum, and the uniformly shrinking largest radius justify this choice. At fixed interior τ the Beta quantiles stay in a compact interior subset of the support, and the Gaussian mixture quantiles vary smoothly by positive density. These profiles have uniformly bounded variation.

8. **CONFIRMED — uniform node tails, interpolation and denominator (§8; U audit 1–9).** For d=(w_min/ω²)tr Q, the crude baseline plus finding 4 gives d=O(k̄_*). Hanson–Wright on ψ gives the displayed deviation O(√(k̄_* log n)+log n) uniformly on the compact. Young's inequality absorbs it with fixed relative slack in the mean and O(log n) overhead. Apply Hoeffding with H_i=(2 log n+i)/t to the dyadic shifts; their summed failure is O(n⁻⁴), and their cost is O(q_i). There are O(log n) nondeterministic dyadic gains. Use the resulting common C₂′ in both cutoff and lower width, independently of the panel Markov parameter. Thus node failure O(n⁻⁴ log n) suffices at O(n log n) nodes. The common panel events, the clipped final node, whole-evidence interpolation and the denominator restricted to ω∈I work as audited. For the actual rate multiplying k(ℓ), restore the missing π in §8's table: κ_min = min_j (1−ε)(π/6)(6−β̄(ω_j)) k̄_*(ω_j,ℓ)/k(ℓ). The printed smaller rate is also sufficient. Use a uniform lower bound on κ_min when choosing the posterior band constant. U-σ inherits the near-scale reciprocal hull margin and all of U audit 8–9's LLN, scale-tail and prior hypotheses. At a design-specific learned scale the reciprocal interval must lie strictly inside the margin.

9. **CONFIRMED — numerical checks and scope (§§0–1, 5–6).** The ordering in §1.2 follows by opposite ordering of f^(1/6) and 1/f. Independent midpoint quadrature with 100,000 equal intervals gives Gaussian ρ_s=0.9920825364 and β̄=4.4302055362 at τ=.05. Independent 160-point Gauss–Legendre integration using the repository's Beta(1,11−10u) and two-normal mixture definitions gives 5.12268 for Beta at .95 and 4.46339 for mixed at .05. At .96/.97 the respective pairs are Gaussian 5.13014/6.23573, Beta 5.84953/6.97106 and mixed 5.16809/6.28111. These are point-density, population-scale values. The sign change concerns the sufficient comparison. Update the historical status lines in §§0,5,6 to refer to §§7–8 and distinguish the threshold calculation from evidence or posterior behavior on the other side of six.

10. **PLAUSIBLE — reverse spectral comparison (§5.2).** Keep the matching result conjectural. An upper cost estimate would need to pay for the omitted boundary eigenvalues, and a lower gain estimate would need a weighted finite-rank resolvent bound. Even matching deterministic spectral traces do not by themselves lower-bound the shifted mixture integral for arbitrary truths. Restrict any proposed converse to a specified truth, score and mixture regime and prove the corresponding lower estimate separately. Neither LO nor U needs this converse.
