## Findings

1. **CONFIRMED — L3.** Write $d=k+1$. In the Horn inequality take $q=\lceil r/d\rceil$, so $d(q-1)+1\le r$. The column restriction and orthogonal projection are contractions. Thus the constant is exactly the proposed upper-bound constant $(3d/2)^d$, including $91.125$ for $d=3$. The Gaussian tail integral has denominator $2d-1$, and the head product contributes $dK$ through Stirling's elementary factorial bound. The stated $\beta_k$, additive $\log(16/3)$, and factor $[16c_k^2/(2k+1)]^{1/(2k+1)}$ follow. The spread inequality is a sufficient budget obtained from an upper bound. Replace the first equivalence in §1 by an implication toward the claimed mass estimate, retaining $n\varepsilon^2\gtrsim1$ for the additive constant. L3 gives no matching necessary logarithm for the actual small ball.

2. **CONFIRMED — shift and smoothing.** Summing the squared Pascal difference by the power integral gives $\delta_A\le n^{k-1}/[(k-1)!\sqrt{2k-1}]$ for $k\ge1$. At zero it is at most $n^{-1/2}$. Evaluate R1 at $\rho=\lambda\varepsilon/2$, including the additive $O_k(\delta_A/\rho)$, and also impose $\delta_A\le\lambda\varepsilon/4$. This gives the stated thresholds, including $n^{1/6}(\log m)^{1/3}$ at zero. R2 with the constant 8 works for $k\ge1$. The oracle product exceeds both applicable thresholds at fixed $V$. The growing-$V$ R1 power $(4k+3)/(4k+6)$ applies for $k\ge1$ only. At zero the actual condition uses $n^{1/4}V^{-1/2}$ up to logs, hence $V\lesssim n^{1/6}$ up to logs. These comparisons test the shift lemma alone.

   Polynomial reproduction cancels the right tail and the remaining error has at most $w$ rows, giving the claimed three mollification estimates. For $k=0,w=1$ define the single weight directly. In fact finding 5 and Cauchy–Schwarz give the explicit uniform kernel bound $|K^{(j)}(l)|\le d^2/w$. Choose the integer width with a sufficiently small fixed prefactor and require $d\le w\le m/2$. The three estimates give exactly $V^2\lambda^{(2k+1)/(k+1)}n^{-(2k+1)}$ in the squared-radius budget when the half-radius shift constant is bounded. At the displayed log-inflated radius, the prescribed width carries the extra factor $(\log n)^{1/[(2k+1)(2k+2)]}$. The smaller log-free oracle width is also admissible and suffices for the theorem.

3. **CONFIRMED — oracle and fallback.** With $L=\log n$, the two squared-radius upper bounds to balance are $A=L^{1/(2d)}\lambda^{-1/d}$ and $B=V^2\lambda^{(2d-1)/d}n^{-(2d-1)}$. At $\lambda_0=n^{k+1/2}/V$, their maximum gives the asserted rate. The spread ratio has zero powers of $V,n,L$, while the shift ratio for the prescribed inflated width gains $L^{-1/(2k+1)}$. Theorem B's flat-section and numerator integrals merely change dimension; its conclusion uses the global (EM) and the Huber loss. If “oracle” means minimizing these particular bounds including logarithms, equalizing them instead gives $\lambda_0 L^{1/(4d)}$ and log power $(2d-1)/(4d^2)$ in the rate. At fixed $V$ this extra tuning still meets the shift condition.

   For the fallback, replace $B$ by $V\lambda/n^d$. At the printed log-free tuning, the certified squared radius has the additional factor $L^{1/(2d)}$. With log-balanced tuning, both the printed fallback tuning and its squared rate acquire $L^{1/[2(d+1)]}$. The table is valid as a table of polynomial exponents. At degree zero $V^{1/2}$ improves on $V$ for $V>1$ only. The frequentist comparison's exponent subtraction is correct if its benchmark is assumed. No minimax or general smoothness identification is needed for this proof. The flagged conversion from Huber to squared loss additionally needs a posterior roughness bound. For a log-inflated spread radius the corresponding tuning window includes $L^{k/[2(2k+1)]}$ multiplying $n^{(k+1)^2/(2k+1)}$.

4. **CONFIRMED — L10-I.** For $T=0$ use polynomial norm equivalence on the full grid. Otherwise Newton's remainder is at most $w^kT/(k!n^k)$. On a window containing a maximizer, $s\le\sqrt{C_k/w}\|\delta\|_{2,W}+(1+\sqrt{C_k})\rho_w$. This proves the stated local mass estimate. Put $H=4\sqrt{C_k}/k!$, $b=(k+1)/(k+2)$. In the middle-width case one may take $K_k=[(4C_k/b)^k H]^{1/(2k+1)}$, and the short case needs only $[H(k+1)^k]^{1/(2k+1)}$. Taking their maximum gives the stated inequality. The Huber self-bound follows from $a^2\le\eta\max(1,s)$. The two smallness conditions and both exponents follow by splitting the two summands at $s/2$.

5. **CONFIRMED — exact Christoffel supremum $C_k=(k+1)^2$.** Here is a proof that covers interior nodes without needing to locate the maximizer of each finite grid. Let $h_s$ denote the *unnormalized* endpoint leverage, the supremum of $p(0)^2/\sum_{x=0}^{s-1}p(x)^2$ for degree $<d=k+1$. For $s\ge d$,
   $$
   h_s=1-\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{s-1-j}{s+j}.
   $$
   To verify this identity directly, put $F_p(x)=\binom{x}{p}\binom{x-s}{p}$ (polynomial binomials) and $Q_p=\Delta^p F_p$, $0\le p<d$. Summation by parts gives $\sum Q_p g=(-1)^p\sum F_p\nabla^p g$ on $0,\dots,s-1$. The boundary terms vanish because $F_p$ vanishes at $0,\dots,p-1$ and $s,\dots,s+p-1$. Thus $Q_p$ is orthogonal to lower degrees. Its endpoint is $(-1)^p\binom{s-1}{p}$, and $\nabla^p Q_p=\binom{2p}{p}$ by leading coefficients. Vandermonde convolution gives
   $$
   \sum Q_p^2=\binom{2p}{p}\sum_{x=0}^{s-1}\binom{x}{p}\binom{s+p-1-x}{p}
             =\binom{2p}{p}\binom{s+p}{2p+1}.
   $$
   The endpoint leverage is the sum of $Q_p(0)^2/\sum Q_p^2$. Each term equals $R_p-R_{p+1}$ for $R_p=\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(s-1-j)/(s+j)$, proving the identity.

   The bound needed for gluing two intervals is
   $$
   1/h_s\ \ge\ s/d^2+(d^2-1)/(2d^2).
   $$
   For $s<d$, interpolation gives $h_s=1$ and the bound holds. For $s\ge d$ put $X=s-1/2$, $a_j=j+1/2$, $S=\sum a_j=d^2/2$. The product in the identity is at least $(X-S)/(X+S)$. This is immediate for $S\ge X$. Otherwise multiply successively, using $(1-u)(1-v)/[(1+u)(1+v)]\ge(1-u-v)/(1+u+v)$ for $u,v\ge0, u+v<1$. Hence $h_s\le 2S/(X+S)$, as required.

   Split a $w$-node grid at the evaluation node into two closed intervals of lengths $a,b$ with $a+b=w+1$. A polynomial equal to 1 there has sum of squares at least $1/h_a+1/h_b-1\ge w/d^2$. This proves the normalized leverage bound $d^2$ at **every** node. At an endpoint $w h_w\to d^2$ by expanding the fixed finite product to first order in $1/w$. This proves sharpness over grid sizes, including $d=1$.

6. **CONFIRMED — F and W′.** The interior interpolation weights reproduce all polynomials of degree $k$ in the knot location. The absolute error is supported between the extreme knots and its squared norm is bounded by $(1+\Lambda_k)^2(kh)^{2k+1}/[(k!)^2(2k+1)]$. The ordered-distance estimate follows because any interval of radius integer $t\le k$ about a point of $[0,k]$ contains at least $t+1$ of the grid nodes (including boundary truncation). Thus $\Lambda_k\le2^k$. At the ends the forward and reflected powers are bounded by $(n-j)^{2k+1}$ and $(j+k)^{2k+1}$ over the same denominator. Taking the smaller also proves the few-knot constant. At degree one $2/\sqrt3$ is the stated interior-regime constant; the general few-knot formula gives $2^{3/2}/\sqrt3$. The zero-degree piecewise-constant argument works. The spline-space sum is direct by $DS=I$, generally oblique. For W′ take independent centered multipliers bounded in magnitude by one, or multiply the bound by their common bound. For $q=(T_0/\rho)^{1/d}$ use $J=1$ below one, $J=\lceil q\rceil$ in range, and $J=m$ above range, where the residual vanishes. These choices give the displayed powers with constants depending only on $k$. Old W has the extra $n^{1/(4d)}$.

7. **CONFIRMED — C1, including the narrow degree-one window.** The W′ radius gives $r^{2k}T_0\asymp n^{k+1}\lambda^{-(4k+3)/(2k+3)} L^{2k(k+1)/(2k+3)}$ for fixed $T$. Old W adds $n^{k/(2k+3)}$. This verifies both lower-window powers and the log power. At $k=1$ the improved window is $n^{10/7}L^{4/7}\ll\lambda$, the old window is $n^{11/7}L^{4/7}\ll\lambda$, and the oracle is $n^{3/2}/V$. The improved margin factor is $n^{1/14}L^{-4/7}$ up to constants. Here $r^2\asymp n^{-3/5}L^{4/5}$, $r^2T_0\asymp n^{-1/10}L^{4/5}$, and the deterministic envelope is of order $n^{-1/30}L^{4/15}$. Old W gives $n^{1/10}L^{4/5}$ in the middle expression. Thus the sharper width is needed by this proof at degree one. At $k\ge2$ either width suffices for oracle localization.

   Outside the oracle, retain $V\lambda=o(n^{k+1})$, a common deterministic L6 approximation and prior-mass budget, $n\varepsilon^2\to\infty$, $n\varepsilon^2=o(nr^2)$, and the head and deviation checks $nr^2\gg\log n$. The lower window by itself only verifies the interpolation condition. At the oracle these checks hold for fixed $k,V$. The flat integration has dimension $d$, the Chernoff variable has shape $n-d$, and $T\ge4$ still absorbs the $2^{n-d}$ factor. Use the smaller inverse-scale endpoint in the negative exponents and the larger in positive costs.

8. **CONFIRMED — C2.** Put $D_n=n\varepsilon^2$. Then $D_n^{2d}=C^{2d}V^2 nL$, $J=\lfloor cMD_n\rfloor$ with $c\le f^2/128$, and the coarse rank is $J+d$. Chebyshev bounds the **complement** of $\{\|P_J\psi\|^2\le J\}$. Range-one score Hoeffding and F give exactly the stated fine failure bound. The MGF test has order $n^{-(2k+1)/(4k+4)}L^{1/(4k+4)}$ at fixed positive parameters, and the truth-shift upper bound has order $(nL)^{1/(4k+4)}=O(\sqrt{D_n})$. The bare powers in §7 omit these logarithms. The log-MGF is bounded by $O_k(\omega^2 D_n/M^{2k+1})$ through the exact identity for $D_n^{2d}$, with constants also depending on the fixed class and tuning comparisons. Restrict the deterministic shells to $\|\delta\|_\infty\le\min(L_{\rm margin},3f/(4\omega_2\bar f))$; intersect unconditional Markov events for all shells at both endpoints with the score and denominator events. The shell volume ratio becomes $(8a)^{d/2}$, absorbed by strict exponential slack. This gives the compact-uniform conclusion in expected posterior probability.

9. **CONFIRMED — Lemma S.** With row-average uniform integrability and common lower scale bound, the discarded endpoint means cost at most $dK/n+u(K)$ after truncation. Stencil absolute sum $2^d$, dependence range $d$, and clipped variance bound $(2d+1)K^2/n$ give the roughness event. The choice $q=k_\tau d_{\rm scale}/2^{k+3}$ leaves $nd_{\rm scale}/4$ roughness. Section volumes use power $d$; the low-scale roughness penalty beats the IG moment of log size $O(n\log n)$ for $\lambda/\log n\to\infty$. All steps hold at $k=0$ conditionally on the same hypotheses. The compact-uniform trend input U follows here from C1 at $k\ge1$. S itself also permits another proof of U.

10. **CONFIRMED — V0T and the mixed upgrade.** On the window $[\Lambda_-,2\Lambda_-]$ the same center works and the spread and bias budgets give the two displayed floor-rate coefficients. On the entire support $n^k\|u\|_1/T_0\le\Gamma/(Tn)$. At the floor the old and improved powers for $r^{2k}T_0$ and $s_K$ in §9 are correct up to logarithms. The old-W upper-window power follows by comparing its radius with the bias bound and is only that comparison. The shape $m+1=n-k$ is correct for the stated truncated exponential prior.

    The coarse V0T proof allows $-\log p_{0,n}=o(nr^2)$. For the sharp C2 upgrade use $-\log p_{0,n}=o(n\bar\varepsilon^2)$, or allow $O(n\bar\varepsilon^2)$ and charge its constant to the shell threshold. These differ because $\bar\varepsilon^2=o(r^2)$. Both named exponential priors satisfy the stronger condition. For a direct transfer of FABLE_A_n56 §3.2, use **one** Markov event for the shell integral against the mixed measure at each endpoint. With $J\asymp Mn\bar\varepsilon^2$, the fine product is bounded by the one at the floor and its log by $O_k(\omega^2\kappa_-^{-2} nL/J^{2k+1})$. The spread part alone gives $(n\bar\varepsilon^2)^{2k+2}\gtrsim \kappa_-^{-2} nL$. This proves the required shell budget. The truth shift is independent of the mixing variable. Pointwise high-probability events indexed by the tuning would not justify integrating a common event.

11. **CONFIRMED — degree-zero obstruction.** On the proposed improved body, take one interior spike of height $H=\sqrt n\,r$. Its empirical norm is $r$, its TV is $2H=o(\sqrt n)$, and $H\to\infty$ at the formal degree-zero radius $r^2\asymp n^{-1/3}L^{2/3}$. This supplies an actual vector on the radial face violating the fixed local-margin cutoff for all sufficiently large $n$. The proof cannot get uniform sup-norm localization on this body, hence its C2 and mixed-rate transfers lack their premise. The degree-zero L3, shift, spline width, Theorem B under (EM), and conditional scale arguments survive. Whether the posterior itself contracts in squared loss by a different argument is open in this audit.
