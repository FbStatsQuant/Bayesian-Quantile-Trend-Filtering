This review takes the statement of L3 as given. References to B and L6 below use line numbers in the two submitted proofs.

1. **CONFIRMED — Fix the law class before taking the supremum** (B 15–19, 91–100, 124–128). Fix ω, b > 0, τ, the upper-margin constants L and f̄, and a positive variation budget V. Require the *same* b in (EM) for every law in the supremum, every coordinate, every n and every real displacement. The displayed quantifier fixes b within an array, but a supremum over all arrays each admitting its own b can approach zero margin. Fix the comparison constants in each oracle tuning as well, or allow the prior constant to depend on them. The proof then gives any M > (2/b)(1.44 C₁ + 1 + C_pr/ω), so the more conservative displayed choice works. It gives no uniform bound as b decreases to zero.

2. **CONFIRMED — State the spread budget with its logarithm and the shift budget for the chosen center** (L6 27–35, B 94, 117–121, L3 16–22, L4q 117–125). Write s = ε². At half radius L3 gives φ(ε/2) ≲ n λ^(−2/5) s^(−1/5) (log m)^(1/5) + 6. To charge this to ns, a sufficient condition is s ≳ λ^(−1/3)(log m)^(1/6) and ns ≳ 1. Line 35 omits the logarithm. Add λVn^(−3) to this spread requirement for the L4 route. For the mollified route add c_{K′}^(5/6) V²λ^(5/3)n^(−5), subject to its half-radius and bandwidth hypotheses. A minimum is justified by choosing the cheaper **available** route and its corresponding G. Keep the volume contribution exactly as −log[V₃(ε√n/2)³]. At either oracle ns diverges and its logarithm is negligible.

   In B, take c ≥ 1/6. The primary bias and log-free spread then both have order V^(1/3)n^(−5/6) at the primary tuning. The fallback shift and log-free spread both have order V^(1/4)n^(−3/4) at the fallback tuning. For fixed V > 0, at the primary oracle λε ≍ n^(25/12)V^(−5/6)(log n)^(c/2), which eventually exceeds the half-radius threshold in B 120 and gives δ_A ≤ λε/4. The bandwidth w ≍ n(ε/V)^(2/5) tends to infinity with w/n tending to zero. Thus both uses of L4q-M are admissible for large n. State B's radii directly in STATEMENT_DRAFT 128. The proof gives the n and V powers at fixed scale, while “same ε² as Theorem A” imports a κ involving a lower secant constant absent from B's assumptions. B 94 proves two oracle choices. Describe a wider λ-window at B 136–138 and STATEMENT_DRAFT 136 as prospective unless its endpoints, radius and uniform prior budget are supplied.

3. **CONFIRMED — The necessary local threshold is the point-density threshold** (B 21–26, STATEMENT_DRAFT 33–35, 128–134). When the CDF is differentiable at the quantile, comparison of quadratic coefficients gives F′(q) ≥ ωτ(1−τ) + 2b. This preserves the strict point-density restriction, including the Gaussian exclusion. It does not imply A4 for the specified secant constant. For example, at τ = 1/2 take uniform errors on [−1,1] and ω = 1. Proposition 1 applies with b₊ = b₋ = 1 since (1−t)₊ ≤ exp(−t). On the valid A2 interval L = 4 the best lower secant is 1/8, giving A4's σ̄ = 2, while σ = 1. Replace the unqualified scale comparison by the derivative implication. If A4 itself is intended, list it as an additional assumption.

4. **CONFIRMED — Tail necessity for EM is valid, but failure need not give infinite expected evidence** (B 23). For d ≥ 1 the event Z > d contributes exp(ωτd)P(Z>d) to the MGF. Thus EM forces P(Z>d) ≤ exp[−ω(τ+b)d], with the reflected bound on the left. This alone excludes polynomial tails. Delete the assertion about infinite evidence. For a counterexample at τ = 1/2 take iid centered errors with density proportional to exp(−ω|z|/2)/(1+|z|)². The tail has exactly the critical exponential rate, yet E exp(ω|Z|/2) is finite. At each u the unnormalized ALD likelihood integrates over c to at most ∫ exp(−ω‖c‖₂/2) dc, by projecting y−Su−Nc onto the orthonormal null space and translating. Multiplication by exp(ωΣ|Zᵢ|/2) and integration over the probability prior therefore give finite expected evidence for every finite n.

5. **CONFIRMED — Both sufficient conditions work** (B 28–85). In Proposition 1, integration of the survival function of the clipped positive part gives line 41 even with atoms. The envelope gives U, whose integral decreases strictly with the envelope rate at each d > 0. At the critical rate U = 1. Above it the local quadratic coefficient of −log U is α(b₊−ωτ)/2. The three large-d bounds give a positive limiting lower slope even at b₊ = ω, where the prefactor is linear. Continuity covers the intervening compact interval. Reflection uses the left envelope without a symmetry assumption. The smaller of the two infima divided by ω gives the common b. In Proposition 2 the uncentered Taylor remainder is valid for Y ≥ 0. For d ≤ 1 its moment factor is bounded by d²exp(c/2), and for d > 1 maximizing z²exp(−cz/2) over z ≥ 0 gives 16/(ce)². Consequently the remainder is at most ω²C_V min(d²,d)/2. L2 then leaves margin c₀/2 under the stated weak inequality on ω, including equality. The numerical remark at line 87 should use a different symbol for the numerically estimated critical scale and the sufficient bound 1/ω_B at line 69. The proposition proves only the latter formula.

6. **CONFIRMED — The L6 probability and finite-sample constants hold** (L6 9–33, 39–63). Take the center deterministic for the given truth and prior. Orthogonality gives ‖δ‖ₙ² ≤ (1/4+1)ε² on G. Each c-section has radius ε√n/2, and the translated u-ball has positive prior mass, so the normalized restriction used by Jensen exists. The bounded increments and Σ|δᵢ| ≤ 1.2nε justify interchanging integrals. The upper half of A2 and the quantile identity give the global upper risk C₁Σδᵢ², using |δᵢ| ≤ δᵢ²/L outside the local interval. Thus B's weaker version of A2 suffices. Jensen applied to the centered integral, then independence at each fixed θ, bounds Var(T) by 1.25nε². Relaxing 1.25 to 1.44 in both bounds, Chebyshev at distance nε² above the mean gives the claimed exceptional probability. On its complement Jensen gives exactly line 25 for any ω > 0. The event may depend on the law and λ, while its probability bound is uniform.

7. **CONFIRMED — The full-space integral and the denominator combine without localization** (B 102–128). Tonelli applies to a nonnegative integrand against the σ-finite product prior. Independence gives the product of the coordinate MGFs. On E split off exp(−bωMnε²/2). For the remaining integral the p,q inequality at line 109 covers both cases and yields H ≥ min(‖δ‖₂²,‖δ‖₂). Orthogonal projection gives the translated three-dimensional norm in line 110. In polar coordinates C₃(bω) is 4π times the sum of the integrals of r²exp(−bωr²/2) on [0,1] and r²exp(−bωr/2) on [1,∞). Translation removes the dependence on u, and Π_λ has mass one. On the L6 event divide by its deterministic lower bound, then bound the posterior by one on the complement. This produces the factor C₃(bω)/[V₃(ε√n/2)³] and the exponent at line 124 without any independence between numerator and denominator. Both terms vanish at both oracle radii. The proof uses no sieve, tests, or sup-norm localization from L7, L9 or L10.
