# Feasibility of the non-crossing extension

**Verdict.** For a fixed finite set of distinct quantile levels, each ordered-posterior marginal inherits both Theorem 2 and Proposition 5 at squared-loss rate \(n^{-(2r-1)/(2r)}\), including the \(O(n^{-1/(2r)})\) order of expected exceptional mass. Constants may depend on the levels and on \(J\). The conclusion covers fixed \(c_j,\sigma_j>0\) as posed.

**Separation follows from Assumption B.** At design index \(i\), all the levels refer to the same CDF \(F_i\). Continuity at each true quantile, supplied by B, gives \(F_i(\theta^*_{ij})=\tau_j\). Write \(d_{ij}=\theta^*_{i,j+1}-\theta^*_{ij}\). Whenever \(d_{ij}\le L_j\), applying B at the lower quantile gives \(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j\le\bar f_j d_{ij}\). Thus every adjacent gap is at least \(g=\min_{j<J}\min\{L_j,(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j)/\bar f_j\}>0\). This rules out touching and asymptotically vanishing gaps under the stated model. A tiny fixed gap can make the asymptotic threshold large.

**Cost of ordering.** Let \(Q_y=\bigotimes_j\Pi_j(\cdot\mid y)\). The joint target is \(Q_y(\cdot\mid\mathcal C)\), and its evidence relative to the unconstrained product evidence is exactly \(Q_y(\mathcal C)\). T-07 Proposition 2 bounds each level's sup-norm error on its localization set by a deterministic \(s_{j,n}\to0\). Eventually \(s_{j,n}+s_{j+1,n}<g\) for every adjacent pair, so the product of these sets lies inside \(\mathcal C\). On the intersection of the single-level good-data events, Proposition 2 therefore gives \(Q_y(\mathcal C^c)=O(J e^{-a n\rho_n^2})\) for some fixed \(a>0\), with \(\rho_n^2=n^{-(2r-1)/(2r+1)}\log n\). The added negative log-evidence is of this same upper order, negligible even against one, on an event of probability \(1-O(Jn^{-1/(2r)})\). This is a high-probability statement about the log cost. In a different model with levels approaching each other, an eventual gap exceeding \(s_{j,n}+s_{j+1,n}\) still suffices given uniform single-level bounds. T-07 alone does not quantify the cost for smaller gaps or actual contact under weakened assumptions.

**Work needed.** The total variation distance between a probability law and that law conditioned on an event equals the original probability of its complement. Marginalization can only decrease the distance. Consequently the expected change in any single-level event probability is at most \(\mathbb E Q_y(\mathcal C^c)=O(Jn^{-1/(2r)})\). This transfers both the upper tail and the lower small-ball bound. All T-07 evidence, localization, shell and Anderson estimates are used in their original single-level forms. The new work is the CDF gap calculation and the conditioning comparison. The common data cause no independence problem: the product is conditional on \(y\), and a union bound controls the data events.

**Estimate.** One to two manuscript pages, low mathematical risk and high confidence for fixed levels.
