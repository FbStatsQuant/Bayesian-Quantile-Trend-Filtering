# Referee report on BQTF contraction, b76ec6f

**Recommendation for Tuesday, 24 November.** Lead with B as a theorem for the single-level posterior conditional on deterministic tuning. Keep the paper’s fitted model and its numerical results. Pair a theorem-covered validation arm with a fixed population check-loss scale arm at the same MAP penalty. Pursue A⁺ at a single oracle tuning as the next proof that would materially improve compatibility with practice. Even that result would leave the hierarchical posterior open. There is a short analytic answer to the scale question for all three benchmark noise families.

## Ten questions a referee is likely to ask

1. **Which posterior actually has a proved rate?** B gives a uniform upper bound on posterior mass outside a ball in Δₙ, for k = 2 and either of two deterministic λ schedules under EM. Its primary squared radius is proportional to (log n)^(1/6) V^(1/3) n^(−5/6). Table 1’s “fixed, any” σ needs “satisfying EM for the true law.” The benchmarks learn both parameters. E-06 supplies a design that can instantiate B.
2. **Is an inappropriate scale causing the benchmarks to fail?** What fails at the population scale is the *expected likelihood ratio bound*. At a correctly specified ALD scale that expectation even equals one for every displacement, although the expected check risk has a unique minimum. A4 and EM are proof conditions. Neither failure establishes inconsistency, incorrect empirical tuning, or poor interval coverage.
3. **How far apart are the scales for the actual design?** At n = 500 and τ = .99, the threshold/population-scale ratios are 20.89, 17.08 and 14.13 for Gaussian, Beta and mixed normal. The often quoted 1.6–6.8 range pertains to the five interior levels. Use the design-wide minimum density and mean centered check loss. See the analytic check below.
4. **Does the target include the ordered extreme curves?** Each fixed level, including .99, has a smooth single-level truth. The extreme experiment also uses a posterior proportional to an ordering indicator times six working posteriors for the same data. Transferring single-level rates requires control of the conditional normalization (for example by joint sup-norm localization relative to the minimum true gap). A finite union bound alone covers only separate fits. No claim for a sequence τₙ tending to one follows.
5. **Where does the roughness budget come from?** Apply the difference operator to the entire conditional quantile curve. Both paper baselines satisfy A3, including the sharply peaked VS truth. Table 5 quantifies why the same n may behave differently across signals. Supply the smoothness argument below so the claim extends to every grid.
6. **Does the paper use the stated smoothing prior?** The penalty in MAP units is λσ. The benchmark uses Gamma(1,0.001) on VS and Gamma(1,0.0005) on S, in shape–rate units. The statements’ Gamma(1,1) and λ around 50–75 describe a different setting. At n = 500, n^(5/2)/V is roughly 770 for Gaussian VS and 35,700 for Gaussian S. E-02 reports learned λ around 2,100 on a VS run. The mismatch concerns the schedule as n grows. Learned λ need not be below the unit-constant oracle in each finite cell.
7. **Does a validation slope establish the theorem or sharpness?** E-02 tests spread, bias proxies and risk, sometimes with the quadratic component projected out. E-06 targets the full posterior 95th percentile and tail mass as well. A slope of a quantile or small probabilities at fixed n cannot establish uniform vanishing mass or a matching lower bound. B alone gives neither a posterior mean MSE rate nor frequentist coverage of pointwise intervals.
8. **Why believe the moment-free claim?** A still needs posterior control outside a fixed small sup-norm neighborhood. Its interpolation inequality is deterministic. A⁺ additionally needs a realized numerator estimate at small σ. The fine-scale Laplace score term needs its exponential moment within its convergence domain, and the polynomial directions have flat measure. A covariance calculation alone does not control that integral. Keep both results explicitly conditional on proof, including their plain-language summaries.
9. **Is the hierarchical model even proper?** Yes for the paper’s positive hyperparameters. In the joint kernel integrate λ first; its factor is bounded by Γ(a₀+m)/b₀^(a₀+m). Integrating exp(−Q/σ) over θ then gives [σ/{τ(1−τ)}]^n. The remaining integral is bounded by a constant times the integral of the proper IG density. The normalizer is positive for finite data. This also works with fixed λ or σ and arbitrary fixed difference order with n ≥ k+2. Propriety gives no rate.
10. **What are the quantifiers?** Use the uniform expected posterior mass formulation with a common multiplier M and fixed class constants. Preserve the equivalence with the sequence definition in one sentence. A fixed arbitrary σ, a deterministic sequence σ₀,ₙ, and a draw from a scale hyperposterior are distinct mathematical claims.

## An analytic check on Tables 3–5 and the HQ numerics

Write hᵤ for the error density and d = minᵤ hᵤ(qᵤ). For **these three families**, the infimum of common σ satisfying EM is exactly τ(1−τ)/d. Strict inequality is necessary for positive b by B’s small-displacement expansion. For sufficiency, each density is log-concave on its support. This includes Beta(1,b) for b ≥ 1. For the normal mixture with common variance v = 0.5, the second derivative of the log density is −1/v + Var(component mean | x)/v² ≤ −2 + 0.04/0.25 < 0, uniformly in u. Gaussian and Laplace validation laws are also log-concave.

For a log-concave h, h(x+t)/h(x) decreases in x for each t ≥ 0. Integration in t shows that the survival-to-density ratio decreases, so the hazard increases. Reflection gives the corresponding reverse-hazard bound. Integrating from qᵤ yields Proposition 1’s envelopes with common exponents d/(1−τ) and d/τ. Both strict inequalities in the proposition follow at any σ above the stated infimum. This argument also handles bounded support by taking the survival as zero beyond its endpoint. No global search over displacement is necessary. For A4 the same number is the limiting threshold when L and η can be chosen small; report the actual slack for any concrete arm.

## Contraction definition

Use supₚ Eₚ Π(outside | Y) → 0 in the formal statements. It matches B’s proof and makes common constants visible, including M, b and the tuning comparison factors. The conventional convergence-in-probability description is equally valid **along every triangular sequence of admissible laws with the same M**. Boundedness of posterior mass and Markov’s inequality give the probability/expectation equivalence, and selecting near-maximizers gives uniformity. A pointwise assertion for each fixed law or a definition permitting arbitrary diverging multipliers is weaker. State the rate on the squared scale throughout.

## Other findings

- **CONFIRMED — λ is a separate obstacle.** For fixed b₀ > 0, λ | θ,Y is Gamma(m+a₀,b₀+‖Dθ‖₁); its conditional mean is at most (m+a₀)/b₀. Markov’s inequality already excludes appreciable mass at either superlinear oracle scale asymptotically, uniformly in the data. E-02’s 0.745 slope supports a more specific heuristic; extrapolating the oracle-window theorem to infer an n^(−1/4) rate is unjustified. H1/H2 change the model and require testing, and s_y estimated on the same data makes H1 empirical Bayes. The compound Gamma scale d₁ also carries units. Tail mass near a good tuning alone gives no bound on the posterior mass at bad tunings.
- **CONFIRMED — tuning window and class need alignment.** A is displayed for an entire λ window and two losses. Its first proof milestone is one oracle and local Huber loss. Keep that distinction explicit in the status claim. For Target 2 specify common constants, a fixed positive roughness envelope unknown to the prior, admissible hyperparameters, and how the λ mixture is controlled. Target 3b with the proposed hyperprior differs from the benchmark’s fixed-rate Gamma. Target 6 with unspecified exponent and tuning is a research question; restrict the proved statement to k = 2.
- **CONFIRMED — supplied evidence has limits.** E-02’s 0.97–1.01 scale ratios concern its free checks. Calling σ₀,ₙ the population minimizer of log σ + σ₀,ₙ/σ is exact. Calling it the established posterior limit for the paper’s entire experimental family presupposes Target 3. E-02’s quadratic-null-space and penalized-direction variance ratios are informative diagnostics, with no direct coverage implication.
- **PLAUSIBLE — ordered extension.** For a fixed finite set of benchmark levels the continuous true quantile curves have a positive minimum separation on the compact design. A future joint sup-norm result could show that independent conditional posterior draws at the levels are ordered with probability tending to one given the shared data. B’s average loss alone cannot supply that conclusion.
- **CONFIRMED — deferred applications.** To invoke B an application would need a justified independent grid model, a common local upper margin, an appropriate roughness budget and admissible deterministic tuning with a common EM bound. For A or scale learning, justify their additional conditions under the chosen design. Unequal spacing, serial dependence and an ordered multi-level likelihood would require their own treatment. No application was assessed.
