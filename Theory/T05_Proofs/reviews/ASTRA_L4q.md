Line references below refer to L4q_quadratic_shift.md unless another file is named.

1. **CONFIRMED — Set, radius and quantifiers (lines 9–25, 88–93).** The lemma proves the bound for the specified ellipsoid and every shift. For radius (r), put \(\rho=\lambda r\) and \(K(\rho)=4\delta_A\Phi(\rho/4)/\rho\). The radius-loss hypothesis is \(\delta_A\le\rho/2\). An absolute quadratic coefficient follows, for example, from condition (C), \(K(\rho)\le1\), together with that hypothesis. At fixed design both conditions persist as \(\rho\) increases, since \(\Phi\) is nonnegative and nonincreasing. L6_evidence_lower_bound.md lines 11–33 needs the mass of **the smaller translated ball** \(u_h+B_{\varepsilon/2}\). Apply L4q directly there with \(\delta_A\le\lambda\varepsilon/4\) and \(K'=K(\lambda\varepsilon/2)\). It sits inside \(u^*+B_\varepsilon\) by the approximation bound. State the smaller-ball bound first, so L6 can cite it directly. Replace the vague set class and shift cap in STATEMENT_DRAFT.md line 103 with these hypotheses, and identify the coefficient at SKELETON.md line 33 as \(c_{K'}\).

2. **CONFIRMED — Shift proof (lines 31–69).** Reflection pairs the two whole product densities. Their geometric mean has the displayed exponent by the scalar maximum identity, and Jensen gives line 41 in the stated direction. The conditional marginal is even and log-concave by joint reflection and Prékopa. Integrating the bound \(2sf_j(0)\) on its central interval probability yields the quadratic cost. Translating one surviving adjacent coordinate cancels its column and incurs exactly the radius allowance \(|t|\delta\) and at most the log-density cost \(\lambda|t|\), including at either endpoint. For the radius comparison, log-concavity of the product law on dilates gives concavity of \(\ell\). The forward secant from \(x\) is bounded by the backward secant from \(x/2\). Since \(x\ge\varepsilon/2\) and \(\ell(x)\le0\), line 59 follows. The central section dominates its average at every radius by even log-concavity of the section mass. Finally the two Laplace factors integrate to \((1-e^{-2})/2\) on the indicated interval after including the density normalization. This proves the stated \(c_K\) without coordinatewise symmetry of the ellipsoid.

3. **CONFIRMED — Boundary construction and A3 (lines 73–85).** One explicit verification of the kernel claim uses rescaled window coordinates \(x_k=(k-\min W_j)/(w-1)\), \(b(x)=(1,x,x^2)^T\) and \(G_w=w^{-1}\sum_k b(x_k)b(x_k)^T\). Set \(K^{(j)}(k)=w^{-1}b(x_j)^TG_w^{-1}b(x_k)\) on the window. These weights reproduce evaluation at \(x_j\) for all quadratics, including evaluation at an endpoint. The inverse is uniformly bounded because the Gram matrices converge to the positive definite uniform moment matrix on \([0,1]\) and each is invertible for \(w\ge3\). Thus each atom has bounded total absolute weight. Its integrated residual cancels identically beyond the window and has size \(O(w^2)\) on \(O(w)\) rows. Both Minkowski estimates follow. Write the A3 assumption as \(\|u^*\|_1\le V/n^2\) in line 73. In line 77 use the same null-space remainder \(\theta^*-Su^*\) for both vectors. This gives the stated unprojected identity.

4. **CONFIRMED — Shift size at the oracle (lines 80–91 and STATEMENT_DRAFT.md lines 87, 103).** The same weights give \(\|u_h\|_\infty\le CV/(n^2w)\) uniformly over A3. At \(\lambda\asymp n^{5/2}/V\), this gives \(\lambda\|u_h\|_\infty\lesssim\sqrt n/w\). For fixed \(V,\kappa>0\) the oracle width in line 88 is of order \(n^{5/6}V^{-1/3}\) up to fixed noise and log factors. Thus even the old cap holds eventually. The proof of L4q itself dispenses with this check. Resolve the different width prescriptions in the statement table by citing the approximation width of line 88, or by specifying a sufficiently small prefactor for the table’s width at the oracle.

5. **CONFIRMED — Bias charge and uniformity (lines 25–27, 87–93).** For the stated width, the charge is at most \(Cc_{K'}\lambda^2V^{12/5}n^{-5}\varepsilon^{-2/5}\). Comparing it to \(n\varepsilon^2\) gives the sufficient squared radius \(Cc_{K'}^{5/6}V^2\lambda^{5/3}n^{-5}\). Line 91 absorbs \(c_{K'}\) without explicitly assuming a uniform bound. Replace “same order as K” with a fresh threshold check at half radius. Monotonicity alone bounds \(K\) by \(K'\) in the opposite direction to the one needed. At the fixed-\(V\) oracle finding 6 verifies this check, and the exponent in line 93 follows. The centred cost \(\phi(\varepsilon/2)\) separately needs the spread budget from L3 including its logs. With the evidence budget \(n\kappa\varepsilon^2\) in STATEMENT_DRAFT.md line 112 the bias acquires precisely \(\kappa^{-5/6}\). For a whole-window claim check (C) where the quadratic improvement is needed, or use L4 where its cost fits the spread budget. For example at the proposed lower endpoint \(\lambda=n^{3/2}\), fixed \(V,\kappa\), the target gives \(\lambda\varepsilon=n^{5/4}\) up to logs, below the sufficient R1 threshold, while the L4 cost fits. Also qualify line 26’s growing-\(V\) illustration: at the log-free oracle radius its displayed threshold requires \(V\lesssim n^{11/14}(\log n)^{-36/35}\). With a log-inflated radius plug that radius into the threshold directly.

6. **CONFIRMED — Elementary verification (line 27).** Here is a direct bound valid throughout the claimed upper range. Since \(a_{\max}\le n^2\) by the column formula and projection, the event \(\max_j|\lambda u_j|\le1/n\) gives \(\|A\lambda u\|_2\le n^2\). It has probability at least \((2n)^{-m}\). Hence \(\Phi(\rho/4)\le m\log(2n)\) for every \(\rho\ge4n^2\). Using \(\delta_A\le n\) from the adjacent column formula gives \(K(\rho)\le4n^2\log(2n)/\rho\). Taking \(\lambda\varepsilon\ge8n^2\log(2n)\) suffices even at half radius. This also avoids using the growing linear term in the draft’s Gamma bound for arbitrarily large radii. At the fixed-\(V\) oracle the product has polynomial order \(n^{25/12}\), so the check closes. The elementary argument supplies condition (C) only. The oracle evidence calculation still uses a sharper centred small-ball estimate.
